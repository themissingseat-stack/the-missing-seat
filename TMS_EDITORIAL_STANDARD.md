# TMS Editorial Standard (central reference)

**This is the single source of truth for The Missing Seat's standing editorial rules.** Every workflow that drafts, reviews or publishes TMS material (website, daily queue, social copy, Civic Navigator, any assistant or automation) should read this file instead of relying on rules repeated by hand. When a rule changes, change it here once.

Companion ledgers: [TMS_DAILY_EDITORIAL_QUEUE.md](TMS_DAILY_EDITORIAL_QUEUE.md) (queue and the Regular Joe comprehension test).

## Standing update 2026-11-10: natural voice, varied rhythm, grounded writing

**Binding for all new TMS editorial packages, website articles, website copy, Facebook/Instagram posts, civic explainers, Something Worth Sharing pieces, revisions, audit-derived public-facing prose, and drafting workflows.** This expands the existing Regular Joe and developed-paragraph rules, rather than replacing factual review or release gates.

1. **Write for a real reader, not a detection score.** Favor specific, grounded, credible, human-sounding editorial prose. Avoid repetitive machine-like cadence, perfectly symmetrical paragraphs, mechanically balanced phrasing, or a uniform sentence pattern. No claim that a style can reliably evade AI detectors, and no claim of human-only authorship when untrue.
2. **Vary sentence length, pacing and paragraph size for meaning.** Mix long, developed explanations with shorter observations, without stacking theatrical one-liners, forced mic-drops, frequent sentence fragments, or artificial "structural chaos." The existing requirement for fully reasoned, natural paragraphs remains in force.
3. **Use varied, idiomatic language.** Prefer specific verbs, concrete examples, natural transitions, precise terminology and audience-appropriate colloquial wording. Regional idioms and conversational turns belong only when appropriate and authentic to the subject or speaker. Some sentences may begin with "And," "But," or "Because" when grammatically and rhetorically justified; fragments are rare and purposeful.
4. **Punctuation should be deliberate, not uniform.** Mix ordinary commas and full stops with occasional colons, semicolons or parentheses when they help readability. Preserve the established TMS release-format prohibition on em dashes and visible Markdown artifacts in copy-ready posts; varied rhythm does not require violating a publication validator.
5. **No fabricated authenticity.** Personal anecdotes, first-person statements, hyper-local detail and personal memories must come from the user, the author's confirmed experience, or verifiable reporting. Do not invent such details or imply first-hand witnessing. Use the user's own source prose as a voice reference when available; preserve their position and distinctive phrasing without inventing quotations.
6. **Remove generic filler and AI-sounding clichés.** Avoid "In today's digital age," "In the fast-paced world," "delve," "tapestry," "beacon," "testament," generic "Furthermore/Moreover/In conclusion" transitions, repetitive "It's not X; it's Y" constructions, and paragraphs or bullet lists with identical bold-label-colon openings. The prohibition concerns formulaic use, not words genuinely required by a fact or quotation.
7. **Finish by editing, not by adding cosmetic noise.** Check the opening for a real news peg or human reason to care. Replace generic introductions, transitions and formulaic endings with evidence-led movement and a topic-specific takeaway. Do not force a tidy emotional resolution where the facts remain unresolved; do not omit an actionable ending when the subject warrants one.
8. **Final voice pass is required after fact and hostile/Regular Joe reviews.** Read the full piece for rhythm, repetition, clarity, accuracy, channel length and authentic attribution. Do not mechanically rewrite an arbitrary 15% or intentionally break grammar to fool software. Fix only what improves the work.

**Priority rule:** Accuracy, attribution, evidence, user-supplied viewpoints, legal precision, platform limits, safety, and the existing TMS double-review and publication gates outrank stylistic novelty. Apply this prospectively; do not republish old material solely to alter its cadence.

## Standing update 2026-11-04: brand identity and article endings

Approved standing update, 2026-11-04. Applies to all editorial briefs, full articles, social copy, templates, checklists, manifests, and automated publishing workflows. Supersedes conflicting generic-signoff instructions.

1. **The Missing Seat** is the publication name, not a mandatory closing slogan or a question every article must answer.
2. **Who Is Missing?** is an editorial lens and may be used only when the particular article naturally concerns missing representation, voices, access, recognition, accountability, or people. Never append it automatically. Do not force a missing-person/seat metaphor into unrelated subjects, including consumer guidance, aviation ticket prices, practical rights explainers, and positive stories.
3. Every article or social post must end with a **topic-specific, substantive takeaway or actionable conclusion**, not a generic brand slogan. A question is optional and must actually follow from the story.
4. **Do not append The Missing Seat automatically to social posts published from the official TMS account** because the account already identifies the publisher. Include publication attribution only when needed for standalone or off-platform distribution. Website branding belongs in the site layout/byline, not in a repetitive paragraph at the end of every story.
5. Retain established requirements: copy-ready plain-text social posts, no visible Markdown artifacts, no em dashes, no decorative hashtags or emojis by default, verified facts, natural paragraphs, Regular Joe clarity, two-stage review, and concrete relevant reader action. Do not require every positive story to end with a grievance or advocacy instruction.
6. **Final pre-publication check:** Does the ending make sense for THIS story without knowing the brand? Does it explain what the reader should understand or do? Is the slogan forced? Remove forced taglines, stock closings, and formulaic rhetorical questions.
7. This is a **prospective editorial standard**. Existing articles should be audited and corrected selectively, without silently rewriting or republishing historical work, and without exceeding the established daily publishing cap.

Example for an airfare story: "Same flight. Different prices. Your rights shouldn't depend on what you paid." No "Who Is Missing?" and no compulsory publisher signoff.


## Mandatory release gate — added 2026-11-04 after blue-wave editorial failure

**Status: binding. A draft is NOT publication-ready merely because the author or assistant says it was reviewed twice.** These rules apply to every new or materially revised TMS website editorial, special editorial, and associated social package. Where an older template conflicts, this central standard prevails. Do not invent review evidence, source checks, deployment checks, or successful publication.

### Gate 0: scope, freshness, originality
- Identify the actual news peg: named people, decisions, dates, legislation, amounts, votes, documents and consequences as applicable. A current-news article must explain what is materially new and why it matters *now*, not just restate evergreen arguments.
- Check for immediate developments and near-term decision windows: pending votes, funding deadlines, lame-duck sessions, court orders, appointments, agency actions and implementation dates. Search for material omissions as well as factual errors. If the story concerns an election, inspect the outgoing government's remaining powers and calendar, not only the incoming winners.
- Check the existing TMS archive, queue and unpublished ledger for overlap; merge or update rather than create a duplicate. Distinguish an article update from a new daily publication.
- Treat third-party criticism or a hostile review as **leads**, not verified facts. Independently confirm every new named bill, amount, appointment, pardon claim, deadline or vote against primary records and reliable current reporting. Do not present rumors as established facts.

### Gate 1: documented evidence review (independent of prose review)
Record **PASS / HOLD / FAIL**, date/time, reviewer, the source URLs actually opened, and precise unresolved gaps. Verify every material proper name, race call, amount, vote, legal claim, quote, timeline and current status. Distinguish projection, media call, official certification and legal effect. Give sources as usable hyperlinks with document titles, issuers and dates; labels alone are insufficient. Verify link destinations. A known missing primary source, unresolved material contradiction, stale time-sensitive claim or missing major near-term development is **HOLD**, not PASS.

### Gate 2: documented hostile-reader and Regular Joe review
Perform a **separate, substantive second pass** after Gate 1. Record **PASS / HOLD / FAIL** and concrete findings, including:
- Could a well-informed critic identify a major omission, contradiction, selective standard, misleading framing, ungrounded conclusion or unsupported allegation?
- Does the reader learn what happened, what went wrong where applicable, who benefited, who paid, who enabled it and what can be done, with the distinction between fact, inference and opinion intact?
- Are there meaningful named examples, dated decisions, documents, bills, amounts or votes when the story calls for them, rather than repeated abstractions or demands that Congress 'must' act?
- Is the prose genuinely readable: developed paragraphs, natural voice, no recycled slogans, repetitive endings, one-line AI cadence, partisan cheerleading disguised as neutral reporting or false equivalence?
- Does the piece provide concrete, usable reader actions tied to the actual issue, including a relevant deadline, decision-maker, official resource or specific question when available?
- Is the package structurally complete under the current approved template: Short Version in **What happened / Who it affects / What you can do** form; approved Seat Status (do not invent one); Pull Up A Chair; Resources by country when applicable; Seat Summary; working linked sources; and a topic-specific ending without automatic 'Ally = Action' or a forced seat metaphor?
- Is the headline accurate and the length justified by new information? Remove repetitive or interchangeable midterm-era rhetoric.

### Gate 3: publication and live verification
- Publication requires **both review records explicitly PASS** and all release gates met. Missing records, unanswered material objections or incomplete sections mean **HOLD**. Never convert HOLD to PASS merely because publication is urgent or requested.
- For a revision of an already live article, preserve the canonical URL where practical, check the article against the live version, update archive/homepage/sitemap only as necessary, and respect the daily publication cap for *new* articles.
- A GitHub commit is not a verified live deployment. Confirm public HTTP success, article text, current metadata and links on the production domain before claiming the article is live. If verification is unavailable, report **COMMITTED / LIVE UNVERIFIED** and the blocker.
- Keep an auditable release record: article/slug, source and link checks, Gate 1 and Gate 2 findings, final content version, publication action, public URL and live verification result. Never claim an unperformed test.

Now Show America What That Victory Is Worth.' article was prematurely marked double-reviewed and committed despite omission of the outgoing Congress's lame-duck window, weak actionable specificity, template deficiencies and a prohibited generic closing. ## Standing purpose and consolidated tracks — recorded November 5, 2026

Its method is independent public-interest accountability and civic journalism. Apply equal evidentiary scrutiny across institutions and ideologies, without manufacturing balance or assigning collective racial blame. Sustain Power & Accountability, People & Opportunity, Race & Equal Humanity, Missing History, and Solutions & Participation, including ordinary people's stories, documented positive results and substantive follow-ups.

Our Stories.** Its recurring features include Wait, What?!, Law or Lie?, Give Them Their Flowers, Our Stories and reader questions, with **The Black American Ledger** as the serious investigative subsection. What Remains.** examines promises and enforcement, extracted labor and beneficiaries, wealth and property, invention and credit, medical exploitation, violence, resistance, remedies and unrepaired injury.
