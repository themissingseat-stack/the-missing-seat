## Main findings
The commission is distinct from the Nvidia/electricity-bill article: it concerns siting, community bargaining, tax revenue and air permits. The NPR opening and coalition account are supported. The project is paused, not permanently defeated. The existing policy demands are legitimate editorial positions, not proof a specific company received a particular tax break.

The central tax-revenue comparison is supported, but its fiscal-year language and the suggestion of a wholly successful Loudoun bargain need correction. A November 18 county announcement reports the November 17 formal pause of certain applications because residents are concerned about noise, air quality and energy infrastructure. That materially qualifies the draft's existing Loudoun framing and belongs in the story.

The October 30 court order identifies the national NAACP and Mississippi State Conference as plaintiffs; the draft's assertion that Boxtown/Whitehaven residents are named plaintiffs is not supported. The Southaven plant supplies Colossus 2 in Memphis. Do not blend it with the original Memphis campus near Boxtown. The court allowed the case to proceed; it was not a final damages judgment.

The 33-turbine/2,508-ton figure is supported as the plaintiffs' May potential-emissions estimate. The overview case page still describes an earlier 27-turbine fleet/1,700-ton estimate. These are dated versions, not grounds to choose an undated current number.

Read the original GW/American University report, not just the news account: the racial odds association is supported; the facility records include multiple development statuses, including canceled/uncertain records. Berkeley's contrasting finding is supported by its own abstract. Neither proves the other false or establishes a particular developer's motives. The price objection cannot be called an admission of racist site selection.

### 1. Short Version What happened
Old:

**What happened:** The four biggest U.S. tech companies plan to spend up to about $725 billion this year, mostly on AI data centers. In America's richest county, data centers pay 40 percent of the local budget. Near Black neighborhoods in Memphis, an AI company ran dozens of gas turbines without air permits. In Maryland, a young Black mother helped put a $5 billion data center on hold.

Replacement:

**What happened:** Loudoun County, Virginia, says data centers generated $1.2 billion in taxes in fiscal 2026 and are projected to provide about 40 percent of its fiscal 2027 budget. Across the Mississippi border from Memphis, the NAACP is suing over allegedly unpermitted turbines powering an xAI data center. In Maryland, a Black mother and her coalition helped put a $5 billion project on hold.

### 2. Capital spending context
Old:

The money is enormous. Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft together plan to spend about $725 billion on capital investment this year, up 77 percent from last year, most of it on data centers and the equipment inside them. [3]

Replacement:

The money is enormous. An April 30 report, citing the Financial Times's compilation of first-quarter earnings, put Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft's planned 2026 capital spending at about $725 billion, up 77 percent from 2025. It was a forecast, not money already spent, and included investment in data centers and their equipment. [3]

### 3. Fiscal years and Loudoun counterevidence
Old:

Data centers there pay. In fiscal year 2026, they generated $1.2 billion in property tax revenue, 39 percent of the county's budget. The county projects $1.3 billion next year. [4] That money has let Loudoun cut homeowners' property tax rate nearly every year for a decade. Its rate is now the lowest in Northern Virginia. [6]

Replacement:

Data centers there pay. The county reports $1.2 billion in real and personal property tax revenue in fiscal year 2026, representing 39 percent of its budget. Its adopted fiscal 2027 budget projects $1.3 billion, about 40 percent. [4] The county says that revenue has helped it lower the homeowner real property tax rate through the past decade. [6]

That windfall has not made the problems disappear. On November 17, Loudoun's board formally adopted a pause on certain data-center and substation applications while it revises its development standards. The county's announcement describes residents' concerns about noise, air quality and energy infrastructure near homes. Rich communities deserve those protections, and so does everyone else. [16]

### 4. Litigation, location, date and potential emissions
Old:

The NAACP, represented by Earthjustice and the Southern Environmental Law Center, sued in April. The suit says xAI operated the turbines without the air permits the Clean Air Act requires. [9] The groups say the 33 turbines could emit more than 2,500 tons of smog-forming nitrogen oxides a year, along with fine particles and formaldehyde. [8] Residents of the Whitehaven and Boxtown neighborhoods are among the plaintiffs. [10] xAI denies wrongdoing. [11] In October, a federal court refused its request to dismiss the case. [12]

Replacement:

The national NAACP and its Mississippi State Conference sued xAI and its subsidiary MZX Tech on April 14, alleging that the Southaven plant operated without required air permits. Earthjustice and the Southern Environmental Law Center represent them. [9, 12] In their May 6 request for emergency action, the groups said the fleet had grown to 33 turbines, with the potential to emit more than 2,500 tons of smog-forming nitrogen oxides annually, along with fine particles and formaldehyde. That was an estimate of potential emissions, not a measurement of pollution actually released. [8] The companies dispute the suit; they argued that the turbines qualified for a mobile-source permitting exception. On October 30, the court rejected that argument and denied dismissal, allowing the claims to proceed. That ruling did not award damages or finally resolve every claim. [12]

### 5. Boxtown comparison
Old:

Boxtown is about 90 percent Black, with a median household income around $37,000. [13]

Loudoun got a tax windfall. Boxtown got a lawsuit to file.

Replacement:

Boxtown, near xAI's original Memphis campus, is reported to be about 90 percent Black, with a median household income around $37,000. [13] That campus and the Southaven turbine plant serving Colossus 2 are distinct sites.

The comparison is about what communities receive and what they must fight for. Loudoun's revenue is documented. The Southaven lawsuit seeks to enforce pollution protections.

### 6. Siting study methodological limits
Old:

A study by researchers at George Washington University and American University examined about 81,000 census tracts and 5,291 data centers. It found that majority-Black neighborhoods had 2.2 times the odds of hosting a data center compared with a white neighborhood in the same state, even after accounting for other factors. [14]

Replacement:

A November report by researchers at George Washington University and American University compared more than 81,000 census tracts with records for 5,291 data-center sites, including operating, proposed and other development statuses. It found roughly 2.2 times the odds of hosting a site in a majority-Black neighborhood compared with an otherwise similar majority-white neighborhood in the same state. That is an adjusted association, not a finding that every listed facility operates or that any particular developer acted from racial intent. [14]

### 7. Do not assert reconciliation or universal class behavior
Old:

Both can be true in different ways. Many data centers sit in wealthy suburbs like Loudoun. The sharper question is who gets the protections and the payoff. A rich county negotiates zoning, tax deals and setbacks. A poor neighborhood may find out after the turbines arrive. The Memphis lawsuit is about exactly that: power plants running before anyone got the permits that give neighbors a say.

Replacement:

Those findings differ. Their datasets and comparisons need to be understood before either becomes a blanket claim about all data centers. The November report also distinguishes large hyperscale campuses from smaller colocation facilities and the benefits associated with them. [14] For TMS, the practical question remains: what protections and revenue will each particular community receive, and will residents have a say before construction?

### 8. Unproved admission of motive
Old:

If AI companies say the deal is too expensive to offer in a Black neighborhood, they are admitting the neighborhood was chosen because it was cheaper to ignore.

Replacement:

If a company says equivalent protections and local benefits cost too much, it should publish the numbers and explain why the people carrying the burden should accept less.

## Literal Facebook replacement, approved
AI brings enormous tax revenue to some communities. Every neighborhood deserves enforceable protections and a fair share of the benefits.

Loudoun County, Virginia, reports $1.2 billion in data-center taxes in fiscal 2026. Its fiscal 2027 budget projects about $1.3 billion. Yet residents there are also demanding protection from noise, pollution and unchecked development.

Across the Mississippi border from Memphis, the national NAACP and its Mississippi conference are suing xAI and its subsidiary over allegedly unpermitted turbines. The companies dispute the suit. On October 30, a court denied their request to dismiss it.

In Maryland, Taylor Frazier McCollum, who was evicted with her daughter in 2024, helped build a coalition whose petition passed 25,000 signatures and helped put a $5 billion project on hold.

Give every neighborhood a fair deal: the same say, enforceable protections and meaningful local benefits.
