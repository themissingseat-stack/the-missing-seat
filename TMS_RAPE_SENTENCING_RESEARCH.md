# TMS Research Ledger — Rape, Child Sexual Abuse, Race and Sentencing

Working editorial: **What Does America Think Rape Is Worth? Women, Children, Race and the Sentencing Gap**

Status: Research underway. Do not publish until the race/offense comparisons are fully verified.

## Core question

Test, rather than assume, whether White male defendants receive disproportionate leniency in sexual-assault cases, and identify where any disparity enters the justice process: charging, plea bargaining, probation, suspended sentences, guideline departures/variances, sentence length, or time actually served.

## Verified national/federal evidence

### Sex of perpetrators
CDC NISVS 2016/2017:
- 94.0% of female lifetime rape victims reported only male rape perpetrators.
- 97.7% of female victims reporting rape in the previous 12 months reported only male perpetrators.
Source: CDC, National Intimate Partner and Sexual Violence Survey 2016/2017, Report on Sexual Violence.

### Federal sexual-abuse cases, FY2025
U.S. Sentencing Commission:
- 1,409 federal sexual-abuse cases.
- 93% of sentenced individuals were men.
- 55% were White, 16% Hispanic, 15% Black, 11% Native American, 2% Other.
- Average sentence across federal sexual-abuse cases: 230 months.
- Criminal sexual abuse/rape: 215 months average.
- Production of child pornography: 283 months average.
- Travel to engage in prohibited sexual conduct with a minor: 162 months average.
- Statutory rape: 51 months average.
- Abusive sexual contact: 62 months average.
- 44% of sexual-abuse sentences were downward variances; average reduction 31%.
Important: these are offense-category averages, not race-by-offense sentence comparisons.

Source: U.S. Sentencing Commission, Quick Facts: Sexual Abuse, FY2025.

### Federal child-pornography cases, FY2025
U.S. Sentencing Commission:
- 1,432 cases.
- 98% of sentenced individuals were men.
- 73% were White, 16% Hispanic, 6% Black, 5% Other.
- Average sentence: 118 months.
Important: child-pornography offenses are not synonymous with hands-on child sexual abuse and must not be presented as such.

Source: U.S. Sentencing Commission, Quick Facts: Child Pornography, FY2025.

### Federal sentencing disparity generally
U.S. Sentencing Commission, FY2017–FY2021:
- Black males received sentences 13.4% longer than White males when all sentences were analyzed after controlling for available personal and offense characteristics.
- Black males were 23.4% less likely than White males to receive probation.
- Among cases with imprisonment, Black males received prison terms 4.7% longer than White males.
Source: U.S. Sentencing Commission, 2023 Demographic Differences in Federal Sentencing.

## Important counterevidence / complication

A systematic review of research on race and criminal-justice responses to sexual assault found no single, simple pattern. Some studies found White defendants received harsher or longer sentences after conviction, while other studies found Black defendants—especially Black defendants accused of assaulting White women or in stranger-rape cases—received harsher treatment. Historical studies found extreme Black/White disparities in capital rape sentencing.

This means the TMS article must not assert a universal rule that White sexual-assault defendants always receive lighter sentences. The stronger investigation is to locate where leniency occurs and whether privilege, race, class, victim race, defendant-victim relationship, charging, and plea structure interact.

Source: Race and the Criminal Justice System Response to Sexual Assault: A Systematic Review (2019).

## Case illustrating extreme leniency

### Christopher Belter — New York
- Pleaded guilty to felony rape and sexual-abuse charges involving four teenage girls.
- Faced up to eight years in prison.
- Sentenced in 2021 to eight years of probation and no incarceration.
- The judge said incarceration was not appropriate.
- An attorney for one victim argued publicly that race and family privilege mattered; that is an allegation/opinion, not a judicial finding.
Use this case to examine mechanisms of leniency, not as standalone proof of racial bias.

Sources: Washington Post, Nov. 17, 2021; ABC News, Nov. 18, 2021.

### Brock Turner — California
- Convicted in 2016 of three felony sexual-assault counts.
- Sentenced to six months in county jail and three years' probation.
- Served three months.
- Prosecutors had sought six years.
- Judge cited youth, lack of criminal history and other circumstances.
Use as historical example of sentencing discretion and public backlash, not proof by itself of race causation.

Sources: Stanford Daily; AP/CBS reporting.

## Data gap: White men raping children vs. White men raping adult women

No current national public summary located so far gives a clean, defensible cross-tab answering:
- White male perpetrators of hands-on child sexual abuse,
versus
- White male perpetrators of rape/sexual assault of adult women,
with comparable population denominators and comparable justice outcomes.

NCANDS FY2024 contains case-level perpetrator sex, race and maltreatment-type fields for investigated child-maltreatment reports. NISVS/NCVS capture adult sexual victimization and perpetrator characteristics. NIBRS contains incident-level victim/offender data. These datasets are not interchangeable.

Next step: build a custom comparison only where the underlying datasets support it, and label differences in coverage, definitions and reporting.

## Required TMS controls

- Distinguish prevalence, reports, arrests, prosecutions, convictions, sentences and time served.
- Do not infer offender race from photographs, names or location.
- Do not use child-pornography statistics as a proxy for hands-on child rape.
- Separate state and federal systems.
- Separate statutory rape, forcible rape, abusive sexual contact, child sexual abuse and exploitation offenses.
- Identify victim race when evidence permits because prior research shows victim/offender race combinations can affect outcomes.
- Compare similarly situated cases before calling a sentence racially disparate.
- Record prosecutor request, defense request, statutory range, guideline range, plea terms, judge, sentence imposed and time actually served.
