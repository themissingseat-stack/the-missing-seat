## US — material policy change: EEOC withdraws 2012 arrest/conviction employment guidance

**Verified facts:** The EEOC voted on October 28, 2026 to rescind its 2012 Enforcement Guidance on Consideration of Arrest and Conviction Records in Employment Decisions Under Title VII. The EEOC announced the immediately effective rescission October 29. It expressly stated that the change neither revives older superseded policy statements nor requires employers to abandon lawful practices previously adopted under the 2012 guidance. The EEOC also expressly stated that Title VII still prohibits race and national-origin discrimination, including requirements serving as proxies for intentional discrimination. Rescinding nonbinding guidance is not a statutory repeal and does not establish that every employer may automatically reject applicants with records.

**People/power/burden:** Applicants with arrest or conviction histories, especially those facing racial disparities in screening, need to know which rights still apply. Employers receive a changed federal agency guidance environment; that is not proof of a new statutory right to discriminate. Do not infer the EEOC's subjective purpose or predict a specific court outcome without evidence.

**Regular Joe action:** Keep the job posting, background check notice, adverse-action letter, decision date and comparator evidence. Check state/local fair-chance law and the Fair Credit Reporting Act as applicable. EEOC private/state-local charge limits are generally 180 days or 300 days where a qualifying state/local law and agency applies; federal employees follow a different process. Contact EEOC promptly; a preliminary portal inquiry is not automatically a filed charge. Source for filing: https://www.eeoc.gov/how-file-charge-employment-discrimination .

**Primary source:** https://www.eeoc.gov/newsroom/eeoc-votes-rescind-enforcement-guidance-arrest-and-conviction-records (October 29, 2026).
**Secondary official contextual page (possibly stale):** https://www.eeoc.gov/arrestandconviction . This page still contains references to the rescinded guidance and older characterizations. Check update status before relying on its details; the October 29 release controls the guidance's current status.

## Germany — consumer credit / buy-now-pay-later

**Verified:** The federal government's October 29 November-rules roundup and its May 19 consumer-credit explainer identify November 2026 implementation of expanded protections for covered loans up to €200, certain interest-free or short-term arrangements and buy-now-pay-later credit; expanded pre-contract information duties; text-form contracts; and a right to human review of relevant automated creditworthiness assessments. Specific contract coverage and transition rules require checking.

**Action:** Save contract, price, payment schedule, fees, consent screen and lender communication. Seek independent advice from Verbraucherzentrale where needed. **Not** proof that all deferred-payment products have identical rules.

**Primary sources:** https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/gesetzliche-neuregelungen-november-2026-2459132 (October 29, 2026); https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/schutz-kreditvertraege-2382528 (May 19, 2026).

**Civic Navigator:** Germany / consumer / loan / BNPL: ask contract date, provider, total cost, automated credit decision, notice/cancellation timeline. Refer to official legal information and consumer advice; do not guarantee refund or rescission.

## Germany — Deutschlandticket consent

**Verified:** Federal guidance updated October 27 says the monthly Deutschlandticket rises from €63 to €66.80 on January 1, 2027. Existing subscribers must actively consent to continued subscription under new price and supplemental terms. It does not supply one universal provider-specific response deadline.

**Action:** Check subscription provider notice; retain proof of acceptance/cancellation; ask about local discounted options.

## Germany — independent debt advice

**Verified:** Bundestag/Bundesrat accepted mediation compromise September 25, 2026. November 2026 government overview says access rules have entered into force. Independent debt advice is intended generally free; limited charges may be permitted if not unreasonably burdensome and not for profit, with advance cost information. Do not promise free service everywhere or that a centre is immediately available. Preserve the final compromise, not the earlier proposed absolute-free language.

**Action:** Use an independent debt-advice directory; check actual fee and waiting time before booking; separately preserve enforcement/court deadlines.

**Sources:** https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/2026/kw39-de-schuldnerberatungsdienste-1216228 (September 25, 2026 final compromise); https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/gesetzliche-neuregelungen-november-2026-2459132 (October 29, 2026).

**Civic Navigator:** Germany / debt / independent counselling: region, urgency, deadlines, fees; do not imply counselling automatically stays enforcement.
