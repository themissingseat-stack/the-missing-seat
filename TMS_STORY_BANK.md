# TMS story bank by theme

Created November 15, 2026. One place for story ideas and leads, organized by the themes Jansen wants covered steadily. It pulls together the leads already in the repository and adds fresh ones found November 15.

**Every item here is a lead, never finished inventory.** "Checked" means the headline facts were confirmed against a named source on November 15. It does not mean the story passed Gate 1 or Gate 2. Before anything becomes a package, run the full editorial standard: refresh sources, check duplicates against live stories, and apply both reviews.

Where a fact came from a secondary source, the entry says so, and the primary source must be found before writing.

## 1. Older adults ("Fine Wine"): nothing published yet

**A. Medicare open enrollment ends December 7**
- Why now: open enrollment runs October 15 to December 7, 2026, and changes take effect January 1, 2027. AP reported on October 15 that a Social Security cost-of-living increase of 3.6% starts in 2027. ABC News reported on October 20 that there are fewer Medicare Advantage options this year.
- Low-exposure angle: free, independent help exists through State Health Insurance Assistance Programs (SHIP). A University of Michigan poll reported by Michigan Medicine found 75% of older adults had never heard of SHIP and only 4% had used it. Confirm the poll's year at the original source before using the numbers.
- Duplicate check: "Before You Cut Care, Did You Stop Paying More for the Same Care?" (published October 15) covers Medicare site-neutral payment. This would be a different story, about the enrollment deadline and free counseling.
- Sources: AP via ABC News, https://abcnews.com/Business/wireStory/new-medicare-season-begins-shifts-arrive-2027-137299296 ; ABC News, https://abcnews.com/Health/medicare-plans-open-enrollment-begins/story?id=137374397 ; Michigan Medicine, https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/5-things-everyone-medicare-should-do-right-now
- Time sensitivity: post and publish before December 7.

**B. Germany: help for older people that has to be applied for (Germany)**
- The lead: several benefits do not arrive automatically. Care allowance (Pflegegeld) from care level 2 in 2026 is reported as 347, 599, 800 and 990 euros a month by care level. A monthly relief amount of 131 euros is reported from care level 1 for care at home. Wohngeld can be claimed alongside a pension. The basic-pension supplement (Grundrentenzuschlag) is checked automatically by the pension insurance, but missing child-raising or care periods in the pension account can cost money.
- Key practical fact: reports say help such as Wohngeld applies from the month of application, not backward, so waiting costs money.
- Caution: the figures above come from the secondary site gegen-hartz.de. Verify every amount against the care insurance rules (SGB XI), the Wohngeld law and the pension insurance before writing.
- Duplicate check: the queue already has Germany items on Wohngeld ideas and the "House Was Full" homelessness piece. Check titles first.
- Source starting points: https://www.gegen-hartz.de/news/zusaetzlich-zur-rente-diese-7-ansprueche-koennen-mehrere-tausend-euro-bringen

## 2. Children and families (21 published)

**A. North Dakota voters narrowly rejected free school meals**
- Checked: Measure 2, a constitutional amendment to give every public-school student free breakfast and lunch, failed 50.61% to 49.39% on November 3, 2026. Supporters gathered 49,338 signatures. The Legislature had rejected free-meal bills, and a lawmaker plans a bill in 2027.
- Angle: a measure that nearly 80% of North Dakotans reportedly supported in polling still lost at the ballot box. What did voters actually decide, and what happens to families with school lunch debt now? Report only what the sources establish about why it failed.
- Sources: Valley News Live, https://www.valleynewslive.com/2026/11/04/north-dakota-voters-reject-universal-free-school-meals-amendment/ ; North Dakota Monitor via News From the States, https://www.newsfromthestates.com/node/436989

**B. When a family loses SNAP, children can lose free school meals**
- Lead: the Center for American Progress reports that from July 2025 to May 2026, more than 700,000 children in the 12 states that report participation data lost SNAP assistance. CBS reports that losing SNAP can cost a child free school meals in two ways. A story carried by the Anchorage Daily News on October 22, 2026 describes school lunch debt climbing after the cuts (find the original publisher before citing).
- Caution: CAP is an advocacy organization. Find the state or USDA data behind the figure and use it only if it holds.
- Related published or queued: SNAP veterans story (published October 13). Check overlap.
- Sources: https://www.americanprogress.org/article/fact-sheet-congressional-republicans-big-beautiful-bill-threatens-access-to-free-school-meals/ ; https://cbsnews.com/news/free-school-meals-snap-changes ; https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2026/10/22/free-meal-programs-vanish-and-school-lunch-debt-climbs-after-snap-cuts/

## 4. America's legal and constitutional history (stories on the topic pages: justice and law 36, missing history 27)

**A. Entry 001: the 1893 to 1894 repeal of federal election protections**
- From Jansen's notes: House vote on October 10, 1893 (Congressional Record, pages 2377 to 2378). The Lacey amendment failed 96 to 193 and the repeal passed 201 to 102. The Senate vote on the Chandler amendment (27 to 38) has not yet been checked in the original record.
- This is the cleanest "named decision-makers and votes" story in the audit work. Keep the three findings separate: documented action, purpose, and knowledge or responsibility.

**B. Qualified immunity: a rule judges wrote**
- Lead: the Jesse Lee Bond article already notes qualified immunity appears nowhere in the 1871 civil rights law people use to sue officials. Fits "who wrote the rules about who can stop them."
- Cross-reference: the Substack piece "The Nine Seats Above the People."

## 5. Seats saved or restored, and positive outcomes (11 published on the positive-change page)

**A. Clean Slate laws (restored rights)**
- Checked: Illinois's Clean Slate Act was signed January 16, 2026 and took effect June 1, 2026, making Illinois the 13th Clean Slate state. About two million Illinoisans are eligible for record sealing, yet only about 6,000 complete the process each year. Automatic sealing is not scheduled to begin until 2029. Virginia's clean slate law took effect July 1, 2026 and has 28 exceptions. Oklahoma's took effect in January 2026.
- Angle: a positive story that is honest about timing. The law passed, and most people are still waiting. Tell readers what to do now (the petition route still works).
- Duplicate check: the queue's reentry piece, "When Does a Sentence Actually End?", is position 15. Compare before starting.
