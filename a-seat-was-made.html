A Seat Was Made

Sometimes making room for someone begins with noticing that they are struggling.

When one student was having a difficult day, her classmates noticed. Instead of looking away, they covered her desk with handwritten notes reminding her that she was loved, supported, and not alone.

The gesture was not organized by an adult. One student had the idea, and the rest of the class joined in.

They wrote short messages of encouragement and covered the student’s desk and chair with reminders that she belonged.

When she returned, she stopped to read them. Then she hugged the classmate who had started it.

Sometimes inclusion begins before anyone gives it a name.

No policy changed that day. No institution announced a new program. A group of children simply noticed that someone was struggling and decided not to let her feel invisible.

Who is missing?

The student who is present in the room but feels alone. The person whose pain is visible only to those willing to pay attention.

Who helped?

One child noticed. Then the rest of the class chose to participate.

What can I do?

Think of someone who may be having a difficult day. Send a message. Make space. Say clearly that they matter.
