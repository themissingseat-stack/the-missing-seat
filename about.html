About The Missing Seat

Why this publication exists

Too many decisions are made about people who were never meaningfully included in making them. Laws are passed, institutions act, money is spent and public stories are told, while the people who carry the consequences are often treated as an afterthought.

The Missing Seat begins with a simple question: Who was missing from the conversation that led to this decision?

We examine the story, the evidence, who held the power, who benefited, who paid the price and what people can still do about it. The goal is not merely to raise awareness. It is to make complicated decisions understandable and connect information to action. We cover the United States and Germany.

The perspective behind the work

The Missing Seat was founded by Jansen Bowden, a Black American who has spent much of his adult life in Germany.

Living between countries, cultures and systems has made one thing especially clear: inequality does not always look the same, but it often survives through the same habits. Institutions explain themselves in technical language. History is acknowledged without repairing its consequences. People are invited to participate only after the important decisions have already been made.

The Missing Seat was created to question those habits plainly and persistently.

This publication does not pretend that perspective can be removed from reporting. Instead, it makes its purpose clear, separates verified facts from interpretation and asks readers to examine the evidence for themselves.

How we work

Every editorial should answer the questions that matter most:

  • What happened?
  • What went wrong?
  • Who benefited?
  • Who carried the cost?
  • Who made the decision possible?
  • What can people do now?

We use plain language because important information should not belong only to lawyers, specialists, officials or people who already understand the system.

Primary and official sources are preferred whenever they are available. Other credible reporting and research may be used to provide context or document facts that primary sources do not explain clearly. Opinion, analysis and unresolved claims should be identified as such.

Sources must support the specific claim for which they are cited. A link is not evidence merely because it appears authoritative.

We use AI tools to help with research and drafting. Jansen Bowden is responsible for what is published.

Editorial independence

The Missing Seat is an independent editorial and civic-information project. It does not speak for a political party, government agency, corporation or advocacy organization. It is funded by its founder, and no outside organization finances its work.

Independence does not mean indifference. The publication takes rights, equality, accountability and human dignity seriously. It may reach strong conclusions, but those conclusions must be supported by evidence and open to examination.

No institution receives softer treatment simply because its stated intentions sound good. Promises are compared with outcomes, and progress is measured against the people who were supposed to benefit from it.

Corrections and updates

Accuracy matters more than pretending to have been right the first time.

When a factual error is confirmed, The Missing Seat corrects it. When an important fact changes after publication, an article may be updated to reflect the new information. Substantive corrections should be made clearly rather than silently hidden.

Readers who identify a possible error are encouraged to provide the passage in question and, when possible, the supporting source.

Reader contributions

Something Worth Sharing gives readers space for reflections, practical lessons and poetry. Submissions are reviewed before publication, and contributors approve the final text before it appears on the site.

Submission does not guarantee publication. Private information about other people should not be included, and photographs are not currently accepted through this process.

What we are not

The Missing Seat is not a law firm, a legal aid service or a political party. We explain rights and point to organizations that can help. We cannot give legal advice or represent you. A listing in our directory is not an endorsement of everything an organization does.

Contact

Ordinary email is not a secure channel. Please do not send confidential documents or sensitive personal information.
