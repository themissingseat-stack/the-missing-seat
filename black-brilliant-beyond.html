Law or Lie?

Open each answer only after you choose.

The Fourteenth Amendment immediately gave every Black American the practical protection it promised. Law or lie?

Lie. The amendment created citizenship, due process and equal-protection guarantees, but enforcement was contested and repeatedly narrowed. A promise in the Constitution and protection in daily life are not the same thing.

See the enacted text at the National Archives →
No Black person served in Congress until after the Reconstruction Amendments had been proposed. Law or lie?

True. Hiram Revels and Joseph Rainey became the first Black Americans to serve in Congress in 1870. Black Americans were the intended beneficiaries, but had no voting member in the Congress that proposed the Thirteenth or Fourteenth Amendments, or in the Congress that proposed the Fifteenth.

Check the House Historian's record →
Once a discriminatory rule ends, the advantage it created automatically disappears. Law or lie?

Lie. Ending a rule can stop new harm without restoring land, wages, votes, education, business opportunity or wealth already lost. That difference—between stopping injury and repairing it—is one of the central questions of this section.
