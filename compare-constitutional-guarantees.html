When Rights Exist on Paper but Fail in Practice

The two systems should be compared without pretending that identical words, institutions or remedies exist in both.

Four questions for every guarantee

What is promised?

Identify the actual constitutional text and the people it protects.

Who must deliver it?

Identify every legislature, executive body, court or state institution with responsibility.

What remedy exists?

Separate recognition of a right from standing, procedure, immunity and access to relief.

What happened in practice?

Measure the promise against enforcement, resistance, interpretation and lived consequences.

The comparison TMS is building

United States

The Reconstruction Amendments expressly granted Congress enforcement power, but federalism, state immunity, judicial doctrine and limits on causes of action can determine whether relief is available.

Germany

Fundamental rights bind all state authority directly. Judicial recourse and constitutional complaint provide important routes, but admissibility requirements, statutory limitations and judicial interpretation still shape practical protection.

Does a constitution merely prohibit the government from discriminating, or can it also require the government to protect people from foreseeable harm?

TMS will not award a winner. It will show the text, the institutional design, the enforcement route, the limits and the consequences—then allow readers to judge whether the promised seat can actually be occupied.
