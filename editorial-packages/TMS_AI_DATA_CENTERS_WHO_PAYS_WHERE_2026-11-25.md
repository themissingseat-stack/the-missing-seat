## The Short Version

**What happened:** The four biggest U.S. tech companies plan to spend up to about $725 billion this year, mostly on AI data centers. In America's richest county, data centers pay 40 percent of the local budget. Near Black neighborhoods in Memphis, an AI company ran dozens of gas turbines without air permits. In Maryland, a young Black mother helped put a $5 billion data center on hold.

**Who it affects:** Neighborhoods where data centers are built, especially ones that already carry pollution and have little say in what gets built.

**What you can do:** Find out what is proposed near you. Ask your county who gets the tax money, what will power the building, and whether you will get a hearing before it is approved.

## Website editorial

Taylor Frazier McCollum did not own a home. In 2024, she and her young daughter were evicted from their apartment. They were living in her grandfather's house in Landover, Maryland, when she heard a developer had approval for a $5 billion data center campus less than a mile away, on the land of an old shopping mall. [1]

She started a petition. It gathered more than 25,000 signatures, led to well-attended protests, and helped produce local moratoriums on data centers. The project, which had been set to break ground in 2026 or 2027, is now on hold. [1] She did not do it alone. Longtime environmental activists, residents angry about their electric bills and new organizers stood with her. But she became the face of it, and she has taken her campaign to Black communities fighting data centers in Atlanta, Memphis and Zambia. [1]

Her question was simple: why put a building like that, with diesel generators and heavy water use, down the street from where people live, work and play? [2]

### Who is getting rich

The money is enormous. Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft together plan to spend about $725 billion on capital investment this year, up 77 percent from last year, most of it on data centers and the equipment inside them. [3] Nvidia, which makes the chips, reported $115.8 billion in revenue in a single quarter. (We covered that in "The AI Boom Has a Customer.") Shareholders, chipmakers, developers and utilities are all being paid.

The question is who gets the money, and who gets the noise, the turbines and the risk.

### When a rich county hosts data centers, it gets paid

Loudoun County, Virginia, has the highest concentration of data centers in the world. [4] It is also often called America's richest county, with a median household income above $170,000. [5]

Data centers there pay. In fiscal year 2026, they generated $1.2 billion in property tax revenue, 39 percent of the county's budget. The county projects $1.3 billion next year. [4] That money has let Loudoun cut homeowners' property tax rate nearly every year for a decade. Its rate is now the lowest in Northern Virginia. [6]

So the idea of putting them in a rich neighborhood is not hypothetical. A rich county took them, set the rules, and collected the checks.

### When a Black neighborhood is next door, it can get the turbines

In Memphis, Elon Musk's company xAI built Colossus, an AI supercomputer, near a long-burdened area of southwest Memphis. [7] To power Colossus 2, its company installed gas turbines just across the state line in Southaven, Mississippi, near homes, schools and churches. [8]

The NAACP, represented by Earthjustice and the Southern Environmental Law Center, sued in April. The suit says xAI operated the turbines without the air permits the Clean Air Act requires. [9] The groups say the 33 turbines could emit more than 2,500 tons of smog-forming nitrogen oxides a year, along with fine particles and formaldehyde. [8] Residents of the Whitehaven and Boxtown neighborhoods are among the plaintiffs. [10] xAI denies wrongdoing. [11] In October, a federal court refused its request to dismiss the case. [12]

Boxtown is about 90 percent Black, with a median household income around $37,000. [13]

Loudoun got a tax windfall. Boxtown got a lawsuit to file.

### Is this a pattern?

Researchers disagree, and that matters.

A study by researchers at George Washington University and American University examined about 81,000 census tracts and 5,291 data centers. It found that majority-Black neighborhoods had 2.2 times the odds of hosting a data center compared with a white neighborhood in the same state, even after accounting for other factors. [14]

A May 2026 study from UC Berkeley's Energy Institute looked at past, current and planned data centers and found they are not systematically located in lower-income, higher-poverty or more nonwhite communities. [15]

Both can be true in different ways. Many data centers sit in wealthy suburbs like Loudoun. The sharper question is who gets the protections and the payoff. A rich county negotiates zoning, tax deals and setbacks. A poor neighborhood may find out after the turbines arrive. The Memphis lawsuit is about exactly that: power plants running before anyone got the permits that give neighbors a say.

### What fair looks like

If AI is as good for the country as its builders say, the costs and the rewards should land in the same place.

- **No permit, no power.** Any on-site power plant gets a full air permit, with public notice, before it runs.
- **Same deal everywhere.** If Loudoun gets 40 percent of its budget from data centers, a working-class county should not get worse terms, fewer protections or less notice.
- **A real hearing.** Neighbors should hear about a project before approval, not after.
- **Put the burden where the benefits go.** Companies that profit should pay for the grid upgrades, the water and the cleanup, not the families next door.

Taylor Frazier McCollum wants a full ban on the Landover project and others like it. [1] Whether or not you agree, she earned the right to be asked. So did Boxtown.

## Pull Up A Chair

- **Look up your county's zoning or planning agenda** and search for "data center" or "large load".
- **Ask three questions in writing:** What will power it? Who gets the tax money? When is the public hearing?
- **Check air permits** at your state environmental agency before any on-site generator runs.
- **Join or start a petition.** A petition from one evicted mother passed 25,000 signatures.
