## The Short Version

**What happened:** The Navy says the future aircraft carrier CVN-81 will keep the name USS Doris Miller, after months of reported internal talk about renaming it, possibly for President Trump. The ship's ceremonial keel is set to be laid on December 5 in Newport News, Virginia.

**Who it affects:** Miller's family, Black sailors past and present, and everyone who believes how a country names its ships says who it honors.

**What you can do:** Ask your representative to support the Medal of Honor for Doris Miller. The Navy's own sources say the upgrade has been recommended, but it still needs Congress and the president.

## Website editorial

The Navy has settled one question about Doris Miller. "There is no intention to change the name of the USS Doris Miller," a Navy spokesperson said on November 23. [1] Days earlier, a spokesperson for Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao had told USNI News the same thing. [2]

On December 5, Newport News Shipbuilding is scheduled to lay the ceremonial keel for the carrier, according to an invitation reviewed by USNI News. [2] When the ship enters service, it will be the first U.S. aircraft carrier named for a Black American and the first named for an enlisted sailor. [3]

### How close it came

In August, CNN reported that the Navy was working to rename the carrier. Three people familiar with internal discussions said the effort had been under way since earlier in the year. Two said there had been talk of renaming it to honor Trump. [4] One source said the Navy had stopped calling the ship by Miller's name internally, using only its hull number, CVN-81. [4] Miller's great-nephew, Thomas Bledsoe, said the family had not been told. [5]

The record shows internal discussions, reported through anonymous sources. It does not show a personal order from the president, and TMS is not claiming one. The Pentagon said at the time it had nothing to announce, and the White House referred questions elsewhere. [6]

The pushback came from both parties. More than 70 House Democrats, led by Representatives Kweisi Mfume, Wesley Bell and Marilyn Strickland, wrote to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Acting Secretary Cao in August asking them to keep the name. [7] Republican Representative Nancy Mace said the Navy made the right call. [8]

### Who Doris Miller was

Doris Miller grew up in Waco, Texas, the son of sharecroppers, and joined the Navy in 1939. [9] In a segregated Navy, he served as a mess attendant, one of the few jobs open to Black sailors. [10]

On December 7, 1941, at Pearl Harbor, he was aboard the battleship USS West Virginia. He helped carry the ship's mortally wounded captain to safety. Then, with no training on the weapon, he took over an anti-aircraft machine gun and fired at the attacking planes until he was ordered off the bridge. [9]

Admiral Chester Nimitz presented him with the Navy Cross, the service's second-highest honor for combat valor. [11] Less than two years later, on November 24, 1943, he was killed when a Japanese submarine torpedoed the escort carrier USS Liscome Bay during the Battle of Makin. Hundreds of sailors died with him. He was 24. [9][12]

### The medal is still unfinished business

Keeping his name on the carrier is not the same as giving Miller the honor his family and supporters have sought for decades: the Medal of Honor.

The late Representative Eddie Bernice Johnson of Texas pressed for it starting in 2001. Representative Mfume has carried the effort since, with bills for both the Medal of Honor and a Congressional Gold Medal. [13] In August, three sources told reporters the Navy was strongly considering upgrading his Navy Cross. One said Secretary Hegseth had sent Congress a memo recommending it. [5] Bledsoe called the recommendation "very positive." [11]

Mfume's bill, H.R. 2335, introduced March 25, 2025, would authorize the president to award the medal despite the normal time limits. Congress can clear the way, but the medal itself is the president's to award. [13] As of November 25, TMS found no report that the bill has passed or that the medal has been awarded.

The Navy has kept its word on the ship's name. On December 5, the keel is scheduled to be laid with Miller's name on it. The next step belongs to Congress and the White House.
