## Website editorial

A worker who thinks discrimination cost them a job has more than one problem to solve. There is the rejection itself, the lost income and the question of whether challenging it will make the next application harder. Then there is the task of finding out what the law actually requires before the time to act has passed.

Germany's General Equal Treatment Act, usually called the AGG, provides routes for challenging workplace discrimination. But a complaint, a request for advice and a compensation claim are different steps. A person can be actively seeking help and still need to take separate action to protect a money claim.

The important fact is in section 15(4). Claims for damages or compensation under section 15 generally have to be asserted in writing within two months, unless the parties to a collective agreement have agreed otherwise. For a job application or promotion, the period begins when the rejection is received. For other discrimination, the statute ties it to when the employee learns of the discrimination. Those are statutory starting rules; applying them to disputed or continuing conduct can require legal advice. [1]

That is a short period for somebody who may still be trying to understand what happened. It does not wait until the person feels confident, has collected every document or is emotionally ready to confront an employer. A right that depends on timely action needs a route people can understand while they are still dealing with the event.

## The complaint and the claim are different

Section 13 gives employees the right to complain to the responsible workplace body if they feel disadvantaged in connection with their employment because of a protected ground. The complaint must be examined and the result communicated to the employee. [2]

That duty matters. An employer should have to address a concern rather than simply acknowledge an email and leave it there. But the internal complaint procedure should not be treated as an automatic substitute for asserting a section 15 money claim. A particular letter might do both if its content and form are sufficient. A request saying only “please investigate” should not be assumed to preserve every remedy.

There is another clock. Section 61b(1) of the Labour Courts Act says an action for compensation under section 15 must be brought within three months after the claim has been asserted in writing. This is a further procedural requirement, not a replacement for the earlier two-month step. [3]

The difference deserves to appear wherever a workplace tells people how to complain. Someone reading that information should be able to see both the route for having the conduct examined and the separate need to protect a claim.

## Evidence matters before certainty

A person does not need to invent a confession to explain why an incident deserves examination. Section 22 provides a burden-of-proof rule: if one party proves indications supporting a presumption of discrimination on a protected ground, the other must show that the protection was not violated. [4]

That does not mean every suspicion succeeds. It means the law recognizes that the evidence can be indirect. The wording of an advertisement, the sequence of decisions, correspondence and comparable treatment may deserve careful review. Their significance depends on the facts. No single example here establishes that a particular employer discriminated.

The missing seat belongs to the worker expected to recognize the legal issue while still carrying the financial and personal consequences. It also belongs to someone whose first language is not German, or who needs accessible information, and cannot easily turn a statute into the correct next step. This editorial does not claim a measured number of people lost claims for those reasons. It identifies a practical risk in the design.

The employer holds the records and makes the employment decision. Parliament set the statutory time limits. Workplace complaint bodies must examine complaints, while advisers and courts have different responsibilities. Those institutions should explain the boundaries of their role instead of leaving the worker to discover them after a deadline.

Employers can benefit from legal certainty and timely notice of a claim. That is a legitimate interest. The cost for a worker can be losing a route to compensation before the underlying conduct has been fully examined. Who ultimately wins a case and what relief is available remain separate questions.

## Protect the next step

The fair counterargument is that disputes should be raised promptly, while evidence can still be found and the employer can respond. A time limit can serve that purpose. The accountability question is whether people are clearly told what action the limit requires, especially when an institution is simultaneously inviting them to use an internal process.

If you think workplace discrimination occurred, save the rejection, advertisement, relevant messages and a dated account of events. Seek qualified advice promptly from a union, an employment lawyer or an anti-discrimination adviser. Ask explicitly which deadline applies, who must receive the claim, what its form and content must be, and whether court action is separately required. Keep proof of delivery.

Do not assume a conversation with HR, a request to a counselling service or an internal investigation automatically stops the legal clock. Also do not assume these two provisions govern every employment claim: other routes, including dismissal challenges, can involve different and shorter deadlines.

Readers who run a business or serve on a works council can make a concrete improvement now. Review the complaint information employees receive. Put the complaint contact, response process and warning about separate claim deadlines together in plain language. Make it accessible, and have a qualified adviser check the wording.

A useful protection should tell a person what to do while there is still time to do it. The worker should not have to learn the difference between asking for an answer and preserving a remedy only after the opportunity has gone.

## Accountability and practical action

Parliament set the different routes and time requirements. Employers control employment decisions and records; workplace complaint bodies must examine complaints and communicate the result. Advisers and courts have distinct roles. Legal certainty benefits employers; workers carry potential lost-remedy costs. The article proposes clear notices rather than alleging that every employer or adviser breaches an existing notice duty. Realistic action: preserve documents, seek timely qualified advice, identify recipient/form/deadline, and keep delivery evidence. Institutional action: review complaint notices for clarity about separate claims.
