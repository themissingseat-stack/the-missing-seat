## The Short Version
What happened: Germany's EUTB offers free independent guidance about rehabilitation and participation, including before a specific benefits application. It supplements the advice provided by the responsible agencies.

Who it affects: Disabled people, people who may develop a disability, and relatives trying to work out support for everyday life.

What you can do: Use the official counseling directory, contact a listed service and describe the decision you need help with. Ask about access arrangements, documents and the next step. Keep any official response deadline in view.

Based on: SGB IX section 32, EUTBV section 2, BMAS guidance, the official EUTB directory, contact instructions and published service feedback.

## Full editorial

A disabled parent trying to work out support for family life does not necessarily need another list of agencies. They may need someone who can help turn the question into a next step. In Leipzig, the official EUTB directory lists Leben mit Handicaps e.V., a service with a particular focus on parenthood and disability, alongside counseling in easy language and disability and migration. That is a named place to start, not a claim about an individual family's experience. [1]

EUTB stands for Ergänzende unabhängige Teilhabeberatung, usually translated as complementary independent participation counseling. The name is a mouthful. The useful part is that people can ask for orientation before they have submitted a specific application. You do not have to understand the whole system before asking how to approach it. German law expressly places this advice alongside the rehabilitation agencies' own counseling duties. [2]

That distinction can matter when a person is deciding what help to request. Asking which office may be responsible is different from asking that office to approve a particular benefit. A conversation can help someone prepare. It does not make the eventual decision for the public body.

## Advice that belongs to the person asking

The governing regulation says the counseling is free to the person using it and is not tied to a regional connection or a particular participation impairment. It also says advisers are obliged exclusively to the people seeking advice, and that people with disabilities, people at risk of disability and relatives should participate as advisers as far as possible. [3]

There is a practical idea behind that arrangement. Someone who pays for a service, someone who provides it and someone who needs it may each see a different part of the problem. Independent advice gives the person asking a place to think through choices without making the funder's or provider's interests the starting point. That is the design of the service, not proof that every conversation succeeds.

BMAS describes EUTB as a nationwide free offer for disabled people, people with foreseeable disabilities and their relatives. Its examples include independent living and assistive equipment. [4] Those everyday concerns deserve a clear route into advice. Nobody should have to package a question into perfect administrative language before finding out whether help might be available.

## Find a service, then check whether it fits

The official directory lets readers look for services by place and filter for video counseling, German Sign Language or Lormen, a tactile communication system. It also allows a free-text search. [5] A filter helps narrow a search; it does not establish that a particular appointment is available tomorrow or that every communication need can be met at every office.

The Leipzig listing provides a telephone number, 0341 92787541, and the email address eutb@leben-mit-handicaps.de. It lists opening times and appointments on working days. [1] Check the current listing before traveling. If you need a particular language, a quieter setting, remote access or someone to accompany you, ask about that when arranging the meeting. These are sensible preparation questions, not assurances about services the listing does not promise.

There is also an important distinction between the local counseling services and the national Fachstelle that supports the network. The Fachstelle's contact page says it does not conduct individual counseling. It warns that personal documents sent there without being requested are deleted. [6] Sending a medical history to the first national email address you find is therefore a poor substitute for contacting the appropriate service and asking how it handles documents.

## A good conversation is useful. It is not the final outcome.

The network reports 215,689 counseling sessions in 2024 and 3,473 feedback responses. More than 98 percent of those respondents were satisfied overall. [7] That is encouraging feedback from the people who responded. It is not a 98 percent satisfaction finding for all users, and the number of sessions is not necessarily a count of distinct people. It also tells us nothing by itself about how many requested benefits were approved.

That leaves room to recognize useful work without turning a service article into an advertisement. An explanation that helps someone make a decision has value. So does an adviser who helps a parent identify the next office to contact. But the larger public test includes whether people can obtain appointments, understand the next step and eventually get the support for which they qualify. We have not independently measured those results.

The funding is public: section 32 SGB IX sets federal funds at 65 million euros from 2023, including administration, networking, quality assurance and public information. [2] That is the statutory funding framework, not a fresh audit of 2026 spending or money paid directly to applicants. The ministry sets the framework, counseling organizations deliver the service, and the responsible benefit agencies retain their decisions. People asking for help still bear the consequences when access or an application fails.

It is reasonable to ask whether another advice service makes an already complicated system more complicated. A useful answer should be judged by what happens at the end of the appointment. Does the person know who to contact, what to prepare and what remains uncertain? An additional conversation earns its place when it makes those choices clearer. It should not become another destination that sends someone away with the same unanswered question.

## Pull Up A Chair

Write down the decision you are trying to make in ordinary language. Bring or describe the relevant letter, ask which parts of the question the service can help with and agree on a short next-step list. Confirm the safe way to share sensitive documents before sending them.

If an official letter contains a deadline, show it at the start. Do not assume booking a counseling appointment files an application, lodges an objection or pauses a time limit. Ask what qualified help you need if the matter requires legal assessment or representation. EUTB's regulation preserves the separate legal-services rules. [3][8] Advice about the route through a system and authority to act in a legal dispute are different questions.

## Seat Summary
People seeking rehabilitation or participation support have questions before an agency reaches a decision. EUTB offers a publicly funded, free and independent place to work through them. Its usefulness should be judged by accessible advice and clearer next steps, with benefit decisions and actual outcomes tracked separately.

## Final Thought
You can start with the question you have. Before leaving the appointment, make sure you know which office comes next and which deadline still belongs to you.
