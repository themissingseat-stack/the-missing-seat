## The Short Version

What happened: Thuringia's former repair programme helped pay for thousands of repairs. Saxony's separate programme reimburses half of eligible costs, up to €200 per repair, after the work and payment. A bill under €115 does not qualify.

Who it affects: Saxony residents considering a repair, particularly those who can manage the final cost but cannot advance the full amount, and repair shops whose customers depend on the scheme.

What you can do: Before agreeing to work, ask for the full cost and check the device, shop, deadline and available funds with Sächsische Aufbaubank. Ask what you would owe if the repair did not succeed.

Based on: Saxony's funding directive, Sächsische Aufbaubank's programme guidance, the Thuringia consumer centre and Fraunhofer IZM's evaluation of the 2021–2023 funding rounds.

## Full editorial

Suppose the washing machine stops and a shop quotes €160 to fix it. Under Saxony's repair programme, an approved claim on a fully eligible €160 bill would bring back €80. That can change the decision. But the shop still needs its €160 first. If you have €80 to spare and no way to cover the other half until the money comes back, you can afford the repair after reimbursement but still be unable to book it.

That is a reason to look closely at a good idea. A repair that keeps an appliance going can make more sense than replacing it, and a contribution can make a shop's quote easier to accept. The question is whether the help changes what households can do, including the households that don't have the cash to begin.

## What Thuringia showed

Thuringia's environment ministry and consumer centre started a repair bonus in 2021. Fraunhofer IZM examined 30,288 approved repairs from the 2021–2023 rounds. Its report estimated potential savings of about 2,970 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent and 390 tonnes of electronic waste. Those are modelled estimates for that period, not weights collected at a landfill or proof that the rebate alone caused all the savings.

The researchers included a harder question: how many people would have repaired the device anyway? In a survey discussed in the evaluation, about one third of participating recipients indicated that they would not have made the repair without the bonus. That is evidence that the money mattered to some decisions. It is a report of what people said they would have done, not an experiment and not a finding about everyone with a broken appliance. The study cautions that the extra environmental benefit attributable to the subsidy may be smaller than the overall estimates for the repairs it supported.

The Thuringia consumer centre says its programme closed on December 31, 2025. It reported about 50,000 approved applications over five rounds just before the close. That broader total and the smaller evaluation sample describe different periods. The first tells us the programme reached many people; the second gives us a closer, qualified look at what it may have achieved. Neither turns Thuringia's old application page into a route for new claims.

## What Saxony's rules ask of a household

Saxony uses its own funding directive and its own application route. Eligible adults must have their main residence in the state. The device and repair firm need to meet programme rules. An eligible bill must be at least €115 including tax, and the contribution is half of eligible spending, capped at €200 per repair. The directive allows up to two repairs per applicant per year. The bank requires a completed, successful repair and paid invoice before the online application. Funding is subject to available resources, so the rebate should not be treated as money already in the bank.

Sächsische Aufbaubank lists November 9, 2026 for the next update to the device and repair-shop lists. That is a scheduled list update, not a promise that a specific shop or device will be added. The funding directive sets December 1 as the annual electronic filing deadline. If an appointment runs close to that date, ask the bank which filing round applies before spending money in the expectation of a rebate.

There is a sensible case for reimbursing finished work. A paid invoice gives the bank a way to check what happened, and it avoids paying for work that never took place. The bank says the minimum bill keeps administrative costs in proportion to the supported work. Still, a shop can make a reasonable repair at a reasonable price and come in under the threshold. And a household with a qualifying bill still needs to pay it. The state and bank set the terms, shops perform the work, and households carry the upfront cost and the risk that a claim may not be approved.

That is where the next evaluation could tell us more. How often does a quote turn into a repair when the person must pay in advance? How many people never apply because they cannot pay, cannot find a nearby listed shop or struggle with the online route? Approved claims cannot answer those questions on their own. A state considering a different design could test whether paying a participating shop directly helps more people, while still checking invoices and limiting errors. That is a proposal to test, not a feature of the current Saxony scheme.

## Pull Up A Chair

Get the whole quote before commissioning a repair. Ask what diagnosis, travel or parts would cost, including what you owe if the work is unsuccessful. In Saxony, check your device and shop against the bank's current lists and confirm the filing window, remaining funds and online requirements. Save the itemized invoice in the applicant's name. If you cannot manage the upfront price, ask the shop about payment arrangements before committing; do not rely on reimbursement as a certainty.

Outside Saxony, use the Federal Environment Agency's repair guidance as a starting point and check your own region's current rules. If you ask a state representative for an evaluation, ask for the number of people who did not proceed after getting a quote, alongside the number who received money. A programme can have a good record of paying claims and still have room to reach people earlier.

## Seat Summary

A rebate can change a repair decision. Thuringia's records show substantial participation and its evaluation offers qualified evidence of additional repairs. Saxony's rules show a separate practical limit: the household pays before it can apply. Approval totals are useful; they leave out people who never reach the application.

## Final Thought

Before accepting a quote, compare the amount you must pay today with the amount you might get back later. If the two figures tell different stories about whether the repair is affordable, that is useful evidence about how the programme could improve.
