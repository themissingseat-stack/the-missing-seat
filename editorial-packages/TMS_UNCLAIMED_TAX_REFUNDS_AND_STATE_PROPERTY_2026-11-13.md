## The Short Version

What happened: The IRS estimated that about $1.2 billion in refunds remained unclaimed for tax year 2022. People who skipped a 2023 return should check their own time limit now. State unclaimed property has a separate route.

Who it affects: Nonfilers who may have overpaid or qualify for a refundable credit, and people who may have forgotten assets held by a state.

What you can do: Gather 2023 records and use the IRS's prior-year forms. Search state property through NAUPA's official state directory for free.

Based on: IRS guidance and March 2026 release, federal refund statutes and NAUPA's state-program instructions.

## Website editorial

A W-2 can sit in a folder for years without telling you what you might be owed. It shows what an employer paid and withheld. To find out if withholding was more than you owed, you have to account for the rest of the year's income and any credits you qualify for. If you never filed, there may be no completed federal return for the IRS to use to send a refund. That is a different problem from an old paycheck that a state office already holds in your name. [5, 6, 9]

In March 2026, the IRS estimated that about $1.2 billion remained unclaimed by more than 1.3 million people who had not filed for tax year 2022. The estimated median refund was $686. Those numbers described that year's group of nonfilers, not what any one person would receive. The notice gave April 15, 2026 as the deadline for that campaign. There is no basis here to carry those figures over to 2023. [1]

## A date to work toward, and a reason to start earlier

For most people, the original 2023 federal return was due April 15, 2024. Federal law generally treats 2023 wage withholding for calendar-year filers as paid on April 15, 2024, even if the money came out of paychecks throughout the year. The refund rules limit how far back a claim can reach for payments. In the ordinary case of a late original return claiming that withholding, with no extension or special relief, April 15, 2027 is the date to work toward. That's a calculation from the rules, not a newly announced IRS deadline for everyone. [2, 3, 4, 5]

Two clocks matter: when you can file a claim and how old the payments can be for the IRS to return them. Filing a late return does not make the old withholding new again. Different payments, extensions and relief, including qualifying military or disaster circumstances, can change the analysis. If you already filed and need to correct a return, or if you received an IRS notice, start with the guidance for that situation. If you are unsure which rule applies, ask the IRS or a qualified tax professional before you wait. [3, 4, 5, 6]

A refund can include more than money withheld. The Earned Income Tax Credit is one possible refundable credit if you meet the rules for that year. Someone who did not have to file might still have a reason to do so, but a credit is not automatic and a refund may be reduced by certain debts. For a person who worked several jobs or moved, finding the records may be the hardest part. The IRS's past-due return guidance explains how to request missing wage information. [1, 6, 8]

## The state search is a different job

An old bank balance, forgotten paycheck or utility refund can end up with a state's unclaimed-property office. The state handles proof of ownership. You can search for free using NAUPA's state directory or its sponsored MissingMoney.com service for participating programs. If you've moved, use the directory to check the states you have lived in. A similar name on a search result is a lead; it is not proof that the money is yours. Submit documents through the official state program after verifying the match. [9, 10]

## Pull Up A Chair

If your missing return is for 2023, start with the IRS's 2023 forms and your income records. Ask which credits and refund time limit apply to that year, then use the IRS's past-due filing instructions and keep proof of submission. For an old account or paycheck, take the state route and check whether the listing fits an address or employer you recognize. You can do the state search while you gather the tax records. Use the state office's own instructions before paying anyone to find property for you.

## Seat Summary

A person who missed filing or moved without collecting money does the work of establishing the claim. The IRS controls federal refunds under tax law; state administrators handle property turned over to them. A timely return can make a federal claim possible, while a state match requires proof of ownership. Neither route guarantees a payment.
