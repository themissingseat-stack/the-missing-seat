# The Nine Seats Above the People

## The Supreme Court can change your life without asking for your vote. That is exactly why Americans must stop treating it as untouchable.

By The Missing Seat | 4 November 2026

## The Short Version · 20-second read

**What happened:** The Supreme Court has the final practical word in many disputes over constitutional rights and federal law. Its nine unelected justices have no fixed terms, no higher court above them, and no fully independent system enforcing Supreme Court ethics and recusal. Five justices can change the rules governing voting, abortion, discrimination, presidential power, environmental protection and whether a person injured by government can obtain a remedy.

**Who it affects:** Everyone living under American law, especially people whose rights depend on courts recognizing an injury, accepting the evidence and allowing an effective remedy.

**What you can do:** Ask federal candidates which specific Court reforms they support. Track S. 5121, S. 1814 and H.R. 3513. Demand recorded votes and explanations for major emergency orders, enforceable ethics and recusal review, regular Supreme Court appointments, and congressional repair of laws the Court has narrowed.

**Based on:** The Constitution and Constitution Annotated; Supreme Court rules and Code of Conduct; Federal Judicial Center history; GovInfo legislative records; the 2021 Presidential Commission report; and the decisions cited below.

There are nine seats in Washington that can reach into almost every home in America.

The people sitting in them can help decide whether you can vote without unnecessary barriers, whether your employer can discriminate, whether a woman controls her own pregnancy, whether a police officer can be held financially accountable, whether a company can pollute near your home, whether a worker can organize, whether a president can be prosecuted, and whether Congress is allowed to repair racial discrimination.

You do not elect these nine people.

They do not serve a fixed term.

You cannot vote them out.

They can remain in office until death, retirement, resignation or removal after impeachment and conviction. Their constitutional decisions can bind more than 330 million people. There is no higher American court to correct them.

And when five of them agree, they can change the practical meaning of a constitutional promise for everyone else.

That is an extraordinary amount of power.

So why are ordinary Americans so often taught that seriously reviewing the Supreme Court is somehow an attack on the rule of law?

It is not.

**Power that cannot be questioned is not made legitimate by wearing a robe.**

Courts can protect people from political abuse. That does not answer the accountability question. Judicial independence and judicial supremacy are not the same thing. Independence allows a judge to decide a case without receiving orders from a president, legislator, billionaire or mob. Supremacy tells the public that the justices must have the last meaningful word,even about the limits of their own power, the adequacy of their own ethics and the injuries their own doctrines help preserve.

That second idea deserves inspection.

## Regular Joe version: what does the Court actually do?

Imagine your town passes a rule. Your state passes another. Congress passes a national law. A lawsuit begins. Years later, the Supreme Court interprets the Constitution or federal law and says what those elected governments may or may not do.

Sometimes that protects people from political abuse. *Brown v. Board of Education* rejected legally segregated public schools. *Loving v. Virginia* invalidated bans on interracial marriage. *Obergefell v. Hodges* recognized same-sex couples’ right to marry.

But the Court has also protected slavery in *Dred Scott*, invalidated civil-rights protections after Reconstruction, blessed racial segregation in *Plessy*, permitted Japanese American incarceration in *Korematsu*, dismantled a central Voting Rights Act protection in *Shelby County*, and removed the federal constitutional right to abortion in *Dobbs*.

The lesson is not that the Court is always evil or always heroic.

The lesson is that **the Court is a human institution exercising enormous government power**. Its history contains courage, prejudice, correction, contradiction, liberation and destruction. Pretending otherwise does not protect the Court. It protects its power from democratic examination.

## The power was not delivered from heaven

Article III of the Constitution creates one Supreme Court. It does not set the number of justices at nine. Congress has changed the Court’s size several times. The number has been nine since 1869 because Congress chose nine,not because nine was carved into the Constitution.

The Constitution gives federal judges tenure during “good Behaviour.” In practice, Supreme Court justices have no fixed term. The president nominates them and the Senate decides whether to confirm them. A vacancy arising by chance can therefore let one president shape the law for a generation, while another president may receive no appointment at all.

The Constitution also does not contain the sentence, “The Supreme Court has the final word on every constitutional question.” Judicial review became established through constitutional structure, early practice and decisions including *Marbury v. Madison*. Courts need authority to decide the cases before them. But the modern habit of treating every judicial boundary as immune from public revision is not the only possible constitutional arrangement.

Congress already has major constitutional powers over the judiciary. It establishes lower federal courts. It funds the judicial branch. It sets the number of Supreme Court seats by statute. It regulates much of federal jurisdiction, subject to constitutional limits. It can create legal claims and remedies. It can demand financial disclosures. It can investigate. The House can impeach and the Senate can try impeachments. Constitutional amendments can overturn Supreme Court doctrine, as parts of the Eleventh, Fourteenth, Sixteenth, Nineteenth, Twenty-Fourth and Twenty-Sixth Amendments demonstrate in different ways.

The Supreme Court is powerful. It is not the Constitution itself.

## “Equal justice” decided inside an institution built through exclusion

The Court’s own historical record lists 116 justices through 2022. For most of American history, the people interpreting everyone’s rights came from an extraordinarily narrow part of the country they governed. The federal judiciary was exclusively white for more than a century and a half. Women, Black Americans, Indigenous Americans, Latinos, Asian Americans, openly LGBTQ people, poor people and many other communities lived under judicial power long before they had meaningful representation inside it.

Representation alone does not guarantee justice. A Black justice can make a decision many Black Americans oppose. A woman justice can vote against a right important to women. No person is required to decide according to a demographic label.

But history still matters. For generations, mostly white men did not merely interpret a neutral system from the outside. They helped define which injuries counted, whose testimony was credible, when discrimination was “intentional” enough, which remedies went “too far,” and when accumulated inequality became the neutral starting point.

Then later courts often demanded that injured people prove discrimination under standards created by institutions that had participated in, tolerated or legally protected the exclusion.

That is not a personal accusation against every present justice. It is an institutional fact that must be confronted:

> **The institution that helped define and preserve inequality also claimed principal authority to decide when the country had done enough to repair it.**

Defendant. Judge. Jury. And, through its control of remedies, sometimes executor.

## Five people can turn doctrine into daily hardship

Supreme Court opinions sound abstract because they use words such as standing, scrutiny, immunity, jurisdiction, causation and precedent. But doctrine eventually lands on somebody.

- When the Court weakens voting-rights enforcement, an ordinary voter may face the closed polling place, the rejected ballot or the district designed to dilute a community’s voice.
- When the Court narrows reproductive freedom, a pregnant person, doctor and family,not a justice,live with the medical consequences.
- When the Court expands immunity or restricts a damages remedy, a person injured by government can possess a right on paper and still leave court with nothing.
- When the Court makes racial discrimination harder to prove but racial repair easier to attack, past exclusion remains embedded in the “neutral” baseline.
- When the Court limits an agency’s authority, the practical result can affect clean air, safe water, workplace rules, food, medication and consumer protection.
- When the Court defines presidential immunity broadly, the decision changes the relationship between criminal law and the most powerful office in the country.

The justices do not personally close the clinic, reject the ballot or breathe the polluted air. Their doctrine authorizes, prohibits or disables other people who do.

That distance can hide responsibility. TMS should not allow it to.

Every major decision should be translated through a simple chain:

**What did the Court change? Who gained power? Who lost protection? Who must now prove more? Who pays? What remedy disappeared? Who lives with the result?**

## The outrage is not merely that the justices have power

Constitutional courts need power. The outrage is the combination:

1. enormous authority over national life;
2. life tenure without regular democratic reconsideration;
3. appointments determined partly by the random timing of death and retirement;
4. confirmation controlled by political actors who can change their own practices;
5. broad discretion over which cases receive full review;
6. major orders sometimes issued rapidly without full explanation;
7. no higher court;
8. ethics and recusal systems that depend heavily on the institution policing itself;
9. immense practical difficulty correcting a constitutional ruling;
10. a public discussion that often treats proposed reform as illegitimate before the proposal is even examined.

In 2023, after sustained public controversy, the Court adopted its first written code of conduct for the justices. That was not nothing. In 2026, it added automated conflict-checking procedures. Those are improvements.

But a code is not the same as an enforcement system. A justice generally decides whether that justice should recuse. There is no ordinary higher judicial body to review the justice’s participation. When the Court changes its internal procedures, the public is still being asked to trust the most powerful court in the country to investigate and correct itself.

If a city council said, “We wrote our own ethics rules, we decide whether we violated them, and there is no outside appeal,” people would immediately see the problem.

A marble building should not make that structure less visible.

## What the Court should be

The United States needs a Supreme Court that is subject to the same fundamental democratic principle it applies to everyone else: delegated power must have enforceable limits.

It should be:

- **independent in deciding cases**, but not independent of enforceable ethics;
- **free from outside commands in deciding a case**, but fully subject to disclosure, investigation and constitutional accountability;
- **capable of reviewing elected branches**, but not treated as the sole owner of constitutional meaning;
- **stable enough to protect rights**, but not frozen for generations by the timing of individual deaths;
- **respected for reasoned judgment**, not obeyed because the public is told reform is forbidden;
- **aware of consequences**, with decisions that identify who loses a remedy and what government-created injury remains;
- **subject to institutional review**, especially when its own conduct, conflicts or power are at issue.

## What can be done now

Not every reform requires the same legal tool. Some can be enacted by ordinary legislation. Some would invite constitutional litigation. The most durable structural changes should be made by constitutional amendment rather than hidden behind a clever statute.

### 1. Congress can create binding, enforceable ethics and disclosure rules

Congress should enact clear rules governing gifts, travel, financial interests, outside income, conflicts and disclosures. Alleged violations should be examined by an independent body with due process, published findings and defined consequences.

The point is not to let politicians punish justices for unpopular rulings. The point is to apply enforceable standards to conduct outside legitimate judging.

**Who can act:** The House and Senate can pass legislation; the president can sign it. Congress can conduct oversight and require reports.

### 2. Recusal decisions should not end with the justice being questioned

When a credible conflict is raised, the justice should provide a written explanation. A neutral review mechanism,not the accused justice alone,should evaluate a contested refusal to recuse. Congress must also provide a transparent procedure for cases in which a recusal leaves the Court incomplete. The absence of a substitute mechanism cannot be used as the reason a conflicted justice must remain judge of their own fitness to participate.

**Who can act:** Congress can strengthen recusal procedure and disclosure law. The Court can improve its rules immediately. A constitutional amendment could remove serious doubt about the strongest external-review model.

### 3. The emergency docket must stop functioning like a hidden side door

When the Court blocks or permits major government action on an emergency basis, the public should ordinarily receive recorded votes, conflict disclosures and enough reasoning to understand the rule being imposed. Genuine emergencies may require speed. They do not require mystery.

**Who can act:** The Court can change its own practice. Congress can require reporting and transparency within constitutional limits.

### 4. Replace appointment-by-lottery with regular appointments

An eighteen-year active-service term, staggered so that a president normally receives an appointment every two years, would reduce the power of strategic retirement and random vacancies. After active Supreme Court service, a justice could hold another judicial status consistent with constitutional requirements.

Serious scholars disagree about whether Congress can achieve this fully by statute while preserving “good Behaviour” tenure. Do not gamble the reform on ambiguity. Use a constitutional amendment to establish fixed, staggered active terms clearly and prospectively.

**Who can act:** Two-thirds of both houses of Congress can propose an amendment, or two-thirds of state legislatures can call for a convention; three-fourths of the states must ratify an amendment.

### 5. Make confirmation a public examination, not a performance

Nominees should disclose significant gifts, financial relationships, recusal issues, major organizational ties and relevant records. Senators should ask how nominees understand precedent, remedies, congressional enforcement power and the real-world consequences of doctrine,not demand secret promises about future cases.

The Senate should adopt stable rules that do not change depending on which party controls a vacancy.

**Who can act:** The Senate controls its advice-and-consent process. The president controls whom to nominate.

### 6. Congress must stop acting helpless after the Court narrows a statute

When the Court interprets a federal statute more narrowly than Congress intended, Congress can often rewrite the statute. It can define terms, create explicit causes of action, specify evidence rules, authorize attorney fees, protect access to court, fund enforcement and state the remedy clearly.

When enforcing the Reconstruction Amendments, Congress should build detailed factual records showing the injury, present mechanism, burden, beneficiaries and need for the remedy. It should not wait for courts to invent the legislative record they wish Congress had made.

**Who can act:** Congressional committees, both houses and the president.

### 7. Restore rights by restoring remedies

A constitutional promise without an enforceable remedy is a museum display. Congress should review doctrines that leave proven constitutional injuries without practical redress. It should create effective claims where constitutionally permitted, provide counsel or litigation support in qualifying government-misconduct cases, protect fee recovery, and require a public accounting when public funds pay for official wrongdoing.

**Who can act:** Congress and state legislatures; state courts under state constitutions; executive agencies through lawful enforcement and settlement policy.

### 8. Use Congress’s jurisdiction power openly and constitutionally

Article III gives Congress authority to make exceptions and regulations concerning the Supreme Court’s appellate jurisdiction. The precise outer boundary is disputed, but the power is written into the Constitution. Congress should stop pretending it has no role whenever the Court claims greater control over national policy.

Congress can hold hearings, identify where jurisdiction rules block or enable enforcement, specify which courts may hear statutory claims, protect access to a remedy and require transparent reporting on dismissals. If Congress changes jurisdiction, it must say exactly what injury the change addresses, who retains access to a forum and what remedy remains. That record lets the public see whether Congress is protecting constitutional enforcement or merely hiding government action from review.

**Who can act:** Congress, subject to the Constitution and judicial review.

### 9. Debate the Court’s size honestly

Congress has constitutional authority to set the number of justices, and it has changed the number before. Nine is a statute, not a sacred constitutional command. Court expansion, contraction and restructuring belong in the public debate because the present arrangement was itself produced by political choices.

Anyone proposing a particular size should state the governing principle and expected effect openly: workload, representation, regular appointments, correction of appointment manipulation, or another stated purpose. The present Court does not become neutral merely because its political construction happened earlier.

**Who can act:** Congress and the president through ordinary legislation.

### 10. Use constitutional amendments when the Court has closed the ordinary road

The American people are not permanently trapped inside the justices’ interpretation. The Constitution includes its own method of correction. Amendments are deliberately difficult, but they can establish term limits, secure a right, clarify enforcement power or override a constitutional doctrine.

**Who can act:** Congress and the states under Article V.

### 11. Investigate whether judicial conduct satisfies the impeachment standard

The House may investigate and impeach; the Senate may try, convict and remove federal judges. The constitutional terms are not controlled by the Supreme Court, and a justice cannot declare their own conduct categorically beyond congressional examination merely because it occurred in or around a case.

Congress should investigate evidence of corruption, concealed conflicts, deception, abuse of authority, serious ethical violations, deliberate defiance of governing law, or other possible betrayal of judicial duty. It should examine conduct and evidence rather than begin with the assumption that anything labeled “interpretation” is untouchable.

A controversial ruling does not by itself establish an impeachable offense. That is an evidentiary conclusion, not an immunity rule. The necessary questions are: What did the justice do? What duty applied? What did the justice know? Was the conduct honest judging, error, undisclosed self-interest, deliberate inconsistency, deception or abuse? Who investigates? Who decides? What consequence does the Constitution permit?

The Court cannot be defendant, investigator, judge and final protector of its own conduct.

**Who can act:** The House can investigate and impeach. The Senate can conduct a public trial, convict and remove. Congress can preserve evidence, compel testimony within its lawful authority and publish findings even when the votes required for removal are not reached.

## What real accountability would look like

After every major constitutional decision, the public should be able to find one plain-language record answering:

1. What law or government action was challenged?
2. What did the majority change?
3. Which earlier precedent was retained, narrowed or overruled?
4. What legal test must people now satisfy?
5. Who carries the burden of proof?
6. What evidence did the Court accept or disregard?
7. Which government or private actor gained discretion?
8. Which people lost protection, access or a remedy?
9. What implementation costs and human consequences are reasonably foreseeable?
10. What can Congress, the president, states and the public still lawfully do?
11. Did any justice face a recusal request, and how was it resolved?
12. What later evidence would show whether the decision produced the promised result or a new injury?

That is not harassment of judges. It is democratic recordkeeping.

## What readers can do besides being angry

Anger is appropriate when power escapes accountability. But anger needs an address.

- Ask every federal candidate which Supreme Court reforms they support,not whether they vaguely “respect the Court.”
- Ask senators what stable confirmation rules they will follow when party control changes.
- Demand passage of enforceable ethics, disclosure and recusal legislation.
- Ask representatives to identify which Court-narrowed civil-rights statutes they will repair and by what date.
- Track how major rulings change burdens, remedies and daily life in your state.
- Support organizations that litigate, investigate judicial ethics, preserve voting access and provide civil legal assistance.
- Submit testimony and evidence when Congress builds a record for rights-enforcement legislation.
- Demand that media coverage follow the injury after the opinion,not stop at the vote count.
- Teach judicial elections and state constitutions too. State supreme courts can protect rights independently under state law.
- Share the receipts. A constitutional decision should never disappear into legal language before the public sees who paid for it.

## The question is not whether the Court should have power

It should.

The question is why any American institution should possess this much power with so little regular, enforceable review of its structure and conduct.

The justices are not kings. They are not priests. They are not the Constitution in human form. They are government officials entrusted with a difficult and essential public duty.

That duty requires freedom from outside commands while deciding cases.

It also demands enforceable accountability for how public power is obtained, exercised and protected.

When the Court protects a person from the majority, we should recognize the value of judicial review. When it abandons a right, blocks a remedy, protects its own authority or treats an injury-produced baseline as neutral, we should name that too.

Respect cannot mean silence.

Stability cannot mean permanent submission.

And “rule of law” cannot mean nine people rule while everyone else is told the law prevents us from reviewing how.

The Supreme Court belongs inside the constitutional system,not above it.

That is not an attack on American democracy.

**It is the work required to make democracy real.**

## The Missing Seat finding

> **Judicial Power–Democratic Accountability Contradiction:** The United States entrusts unelected, life-tenured justices with final practical authority over many constitutional rights and remedies, while providing no regular appointment cycle, no higher judicial review and no fully independent enforcement mechanism for Supreme Court ethics and recusal. The people subject to the Court’s power are told that reviewing its structure threatens the rule of law, although unreviewable public power is itself a threat to the rule of law.

## Regular Joe takeaway

The Supreme Court can protect you from government. It can also decide that the law will not protect you, that you cannot prove your injury, or that no court can give you a remedy. You cannot vote a justice out. That is why paying attention only when a famous case is decided is too late. Americans must watch who selects the justices, what rules govern them, how they use emergency power, which evidence they accept, who loses a remedy and whether elected officials use the constitutional powers they still possess.

## The six-question TMS test

### What happened?

Over time, the Supreme Court became the final practical authority in many disputes about constitutional meaning. Nine justices serve without fixed terms, choose most of the cases they hear, and can invalidate or narrow laws enacted by elected governments. The Court adopted its first written ethics code in 2023 and introduced automated conflict checking in 2026, but it still lacks a fully independent enforcement system for Supreme Court ethics and contested recusals.

### Why did the promise fail in practice?

The constitutional promise of separated powers did not produce an equally strong check on the branch that interprets the limits of every branch, including its own. Congress possesses powers over Court structure, jurisdiction, legislation, investigation, impeachment and constitutional amendments. Public debate nevertheless often treats the Court’s present arrangement as untouchable.

### Who benefited?

The direct institutional beneficiary is the Supreme Court itself, which retains broad control over its docket, internal conduct and constitutional doctrine. Governments, organizations and private interests also benefit when the Court enlarges their discretion, blocks a remedy, raises a claimant’s proof burden or invalidates a regulation they oppose. The beneficiary changes by case and must be documented rather than assumed.

### Who paid?

People affected by each ruling carry the material cost: the rejected voter, the patient denied care, the worker without a remedy, the community living with pollution, the person injured by public power and the generation required to prove again that an old injury did not disappear merely because the law stopped naming it. Taxpayers also finance the Court, government litigation and implementation of the resulting legal rule.

### Which decisions made it possible?

Presidents selected nominees. Senates confirmed them or changed how vacancies were handled. Congress set the Court at nine seats, constructed much of its jurisdiction, and often declined to repair statutes or establish stronger ethics enforcement. The Court developed and applied doctrines governing judicial review, standing, immunity, proof and remedies. State officials and lower courts then implemented those rules. Responsibility is distributed, but it is not ownerless.

### What can readers realistically do now?

Readers can demand specific commitments rather than slogans: a vote on enforceable ethics legislation, written and reviewable recusal decisions, explanations and recorded votes for major emergency orders, regular appointments through an eighteen-year active-service amendment, congressional hearings following decisions that remove remedies, and legislation restoring claims the Court narrowed. Readers can preserve examples showing how a doctrine changed real life and submit that evidence to congressional committees, state lawmakers, journalists and public-interest organizations.

## Oversight without enforceable restoration

The Court’s 2023 code and 2026 conflict software show that the institution received notice of ethics and recusal concerns and took action. The remaining accountability question is what concrete relief follows when a violation is alleged or established. A written code, disclosure form or automated warning is oversight machinery. Restoration requires an independent finding, correction of the affected proceeding where legally possible, discipline or another defined consequence, disclosure to the parties and a prevention measure. Without those links, the record may show procedural improvement without showing that injured people received repair.

## Prior-work and originality check

This package does not claim to have discovered concerns about judicial supremacy or Supreme Court reform. The 2021 Presidential Commission examined Court expansion, term limits, jurisdiction, transparency and ethics. Scholars including Larry Kramer, Keith Whittington, Mark Tushnet, Ryan Doerfler and Samuel Moyn have challenged judicial supremacy or proposed ways to democratize or reduce the Court’s power. Legal scholars and civil-rights writers have separately documented how standing, immunity, proof burdens and restricted remedies can leave rights unenforced.

The TMS contribution is the combined Regular Joe accountability chain:

**appointment and tenure → doctrine → proof burden → remedy → human injury → beneficiary → available correction → action or inaction after notice**

It also connects Court reform to TMS’s permanent injury-and-beneficiary ledger and oversight-without-restoration test. The original synthesis is not “the Court is powerful.” It is the demand that every major decision be followed through to the person who carried the burden, the institution that retained the benefit, the official who could correct it and the restoration that did or did not arrive.

## How we checked this

We used the constitutional text, Congress’s Constitution Annotated, official Supreme Court materials, Federal Judicial Center history, GovInfo bill records, the 2021 Presidential Commission report and published Court opinions. The current ethics-bill status in this package is limited to the latest official actions confirmed in the cited records. It must be refreshed immediately before publication.

The article distinguishes three kinds of claims. Historical dates, institutional rules, bill actions and case holdings are factual claims. Statements about who benefits in a particular case require case-specific evidence and are not assumed from ideology. The conclusion that the existing combination of tenure, remedial authority and internal ethics control creates a democratic-accountability contradiction is TMS analysis.

No new interviews were conducted. We did not treat the absence of a disciplinary finding as proof that no misconduct occurred, or an allegation as proof that misconduct did occur. We did not presume that judicial conduct becomes immune from investigation merely because it appears in or around a judicial decision.

## Social copy

### Facebook

Nine unelected people can change the practical meaning of a constitutional right for more than 330 million Americans. They serve without fixed terms. You cannot vote them out. There is no higher American court above them, and the Supreme Court still lacks a fully independent system for enforcing its own ethics and recusal rules.

That power reaches ordinary life. Court doctrine helps determine whether a voter can challenge a restriction, whether a person injured by government receives a remedy, whether Congress may repair discrimination, whether a federal agency may protect workers or the environment, and how much immunity surrounds a president or public official.

The current arrangement is not untouchable. Congress sets the number of seats. It controls much of the Court’s statutory structure and jurisdiction. Nine unelected people can change the practical meaning of a constitutional right for more than 330 million Americans. They serve without fixed terms. You cannot vote them out.
