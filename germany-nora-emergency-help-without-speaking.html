GERMANY • DISABILITY ACCESS • A SEAT WAS MADE

Germany Built a Way to Call for Help Without Speaking. Set It Up Before You Need It.

By Jansen · November 11, 2026

The Short Version · 20-second read

What happened: Germany’s states built an official app that sends an emergency request to dispatch without requiring a spoken conversation. North Rhine-Westphalia’s five-year report says around 40,000 requests were answered.

Who it affects: People who cannot hear or speak on a voice call, or who cannot safely speak in a particular emergency.

What you can do: Get nora through the official site, complete registration while you have time, and use Notruf-Demo to practise without contacting a real dispatcher.

Based on: North Rhine-Westphalia’s interior ministry, the app operator and the federal health portal.

Seat Affected: Deaf and hard-of-hearing people, people with speech disabilities and others who cannot safely speak during an emergency · Seat Status: A documented opportunity opened; the wider lesson needs careful teaching

A person can know exactly where they are and what has happened and still be unable to tell a dispatcher over the phone. Imagine spotting a fire in your building and being able to type your address, but being unable to speak it. The problem starts before anyone decides how to respond. It starts with what the service expects a person to be able to do just to ask for help.

Germany’s states made another route through nora. You answer short questions in the app and send an emergency request to the appropriate control centre. The app can send your location when you allow it, and the operator’s instructions explain how to enter it manually. After sending, you can exchange messages with dispatch. That gives a person a way to describe the emergency and answer follow-up questions without speaking. The service still needs an internet connection. [3, 4, 5, 8]

The North Rhine-Westphalia interior ministry coordinates nora for all the states. Its September 2026 anniversary report says nearly 300 control centres can receive requests through the app. The ministry reports about 40,000 requests answered since launch and around 700,000 registrations. Those are useful measures of reach and use. They do not tell us how many users were Deaf, how many calls came from different people, how quickly help arrived or how many people gave up before they could register. A national system has done something concrete by letting people get through without a voice call. The next useful numbers would tell us who still cannot. [1]

The work happens before the emergency

The ministry says false reports led it to stop accepting new registrations in September 2023. People who had already registered could keep using nora, according to the ministry. Registration reopened on September 23, 2024, after changes to the process. There is a reason to keep false calls from taking a dispatcher's time. There is also a real cost when a person who needs another way to call has to wait or navigate more steps before they can use it. [2]

Start with the mobile number and text-message verification. Follow the prompts the app gives you. The operator’s German overview presents the postal identity service POSTIDENT as required, while its FAQ and English instructions describe cases where a confirmation call or identity check is added. If you are prompted to take the confirmation call, the FAQ says you do not have to speak or understand the recording. If registration is incomplete, downloading the app by itself will not let you send an emergency request. This is exactly the kind of detail the states can make easier by publishing consistent instructions and showing how many people finish each step. [3, 4, 5]

Pull Up A Chair

If you live in Germany and nora would help you, download it from the official site and finish registering before you need it. Then open Notruf-Demo from the app menu. It lets you practise without alerting a control centre. Do not send a real request to try it out. If registration stalls, use the operator’s support page for help while there is time. The support form is not an emergency channel. The standard numbers remain 112 for fire or medical rescue and 110 for police; the operator also points existing Tess Relay users to their relay service. Work out a backup you can actually use if your phone has no internet. [4, 6, 7, 8]

For a family, workplace or community group, the useful check is simple: can the person who needs this option finish setting it up and reach the demo on their own device? Do that together while the phone is charged, the connection works and there is time to fix a problem.

Seat Summary

People who cannot use a spoken call gain a direct route to dispatch once registered and connected. The states and their coordination office control setup and the instructions; dispatch centres receive requests. People who cannot finish setup or get online still carry the risk of being unable to use this route. The ministry counts answered requests and registrations; this story does not infer rescue outcomes or the cost of the programme from those figures.

How we checked this

Checked November 10 and refreshed November 11, 2026. The operator’s English overview still presents POSTIDENT identification as a current requirement, while other official and press descriptions add a confirmation call at first registration. The latest figures cited are the ministry’s September anniversary counts, not November totals or an independent outcome evaluation. The opening scene is explicitly hypothetical. No original interview, device registration, live emergency request or real dispatch test was conducted. Official descriptions differ on when extra verification is prompted; the article directs readers to the app’s current prompts without promising a particular route.
