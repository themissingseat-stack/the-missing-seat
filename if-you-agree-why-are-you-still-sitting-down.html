COURAGE, ALLYSHIP & PUBLIC RESPONSIBILITY • JULY 26, 2026

If You Agree… Why Are You Still Sitting Down?

Reading is not action. Agreement is not courage. What happens after the truth reaches us?

Seat Affected: Active civic courage
Seat Status: Frequently acknowledged, too often left empty
Location: Everywhere
Theme: Allyship, responsibility, community and action

People read.

They pause. They react. They agree.

Then many continue scrolling.

The Missing Seat was created to present facts and ask questions—not to bully people into repeating the correct words, and not to force every reader into public performance.

But there is a question we cannot avoid:

If you agree that something is unjust, what changes because you know?

Awareness matters. It is often the first step. A person cannot challenge what they do not recognize.

But awareness can also become a comfortable resting place. We learn enough to feel informed, sympathetic or disturbed—without accepting any responsibility for what happens next.

Silence Is Not Always Neutral

There are real reasons people remain quiet.

They fear conflict at work. They worry about family. They do not want to say the wrong thing. They may lack time, money, confidence or expertise. Some are exhausted by a public culture that turns every imperfect sentence into evidence of moral failure.

Those concerns deserve understanding.

But understanding fear does not make its consequences disappear.

When people who privately oppose an injustice refuse to challenge it publicly, the loudest and most organized voices gain more power than their numbers may deserve.

Silence may feel neutral to the silent person.

To the person carrying the burden, it can feel like abandonment.

Ally Is a Verb

Being an ally does not require leading someone else’s struggle, speaking over affected people or making yourself the center of their pain.

It means refusing to make the vulnerable person carry every cost alone.

Action can be public or private:

Correct a false claim in the room where it was made.
Ask who was excluded from a decision.
Call an elected representative.
Attend a school-board meeting.
Support credible local journalism.
Buy from a business denied the usual access.
Share verified information.
Offer expertise, transportation, childcare, money or time.
Stand beside the person who is always expected to stand alone.

No single action repairs a system.

But systems survive through repeated human choices—and they change through repeated human choices too.

What Courage Is Not

Courage is not cruelty.

It is not humiliating people who are learning. It is not demanding ideological perfection. It is not violence, hatred or replacing one hierarchy with another.

The goal is not to make more people afraid.

The goal is to make fewer people disposable.

That requires reason, human decency and the willingness to act even when action brings no applause.

Who Is Missing?

The person who agrees privately but disappears when support becomes visible.

The colleague who sees the pattern but leaves the targeted employee to document it alone.

The friend who says, “You know how I feel,” but will not say it where the harm occurs.

And the reader who believes the question is fair but assumes somebody else will take the seat.

One Action

Choose one verified story you care about.

Do one concrete thing before the day ends: send the email, make the call, attend the meeting, donate, share the evidence with context, ask the difficult question or check on the person affected.

Not everything. One thing.

Then do not announce it unless announcing it helps the work.

Return to the Seat

The Missing Seat will return to the issues we cover. We will ask whether conditions improved, worsened or simply disappeared from public attention.

We ask the same of our readers.

Did the story become another fact you carried?

Or did it change what you were willing to do?

Agreement is valuable.

But an empty seat can agree with everything.

If you agree—why are you still sitting down?

Who is missing?
Ally = Action.
Take Your Seat. 🪑

Editorial Note

This is a TMS reflection and call to civic action, not a factual allegation about any individual reader. It intentionally recognizes legitimate barriers to public action while asking readers to consider the consequences of inaction.
