Who is missing?

The Missing Seat examines the people, evidence and consequences that disappear when public debates are reduced to the voices already in the room. We publish reported editorials on rights, history, government and accountability, and point readers toward practical ways to respond.

The Missing Seat

Public decisions look different when we ask who was never given a seat at the table.

That question guides our reporting. We look at the record, the people affected, who benefited, who carried the burden and what happened afterward. The point isn't to manufacture outrage. It's to make important information harder to ignore.

Why the chair?

Because power has a seating chart.

Decisions get made in government, schools, boardrooms, hospitals and neighborhoods. The people affected by those decisions aren't always represented when they are made.

Our chair is a reminder to ask who isn't there, whose evidence isn't being heard and who will live with the consequences.
