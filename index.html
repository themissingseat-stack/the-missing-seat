The Part We Skip

People rarely act before they understand.

We ask people to care, speak up, vote, volunteer, or change their minds. But sometimes we haven't even explained what happened. Start there. Say what changed, who it affects, and what someone can actually do. Then give them room to decide.

Deutsche Übersetzung

Menschen handeln selten, bevor sie etwas verstanden haben.

Wir fordern Menschen auf, sich zu kümmern, ihre Stimme zu erheben, wählen zu gehen, mitzuhelfen oder ihre Meinung zu ändern. Manchmal haben wir aber noch nicht einmal erklärt, was passiert ist. Fang damit an: Was hat sich geändert? Wen betrifft es? Was kann man tatsächlich tun? Und dann lass die Menschen selbst entscheiden.

About The Missing Seat

The story changes when we ask who wasn't in the room.

The Missing Seat finds what you weren’t told: the decisions, laws, spending and rights that shape your life, explained plainly, with what you can do about it.

You have more right to know, and more say, than you’ve been led to believe.

The Missing Seat is an independent public-interest editorial platform built around a simple question: Who is missing?

We look beyond the loudest argument and examine the record, the people affected, who benefited, who carried the burden and what happened afterward.

We aren't interested in outrage for its own sake. We want readers to understand what happened well enough to decide what deserves their attention and what they can actually do next.

Why the chair?

Because power has a seating chart.

Decisions get made in government, schools, boardrooms, hospitals and neighborhoods. The people affected by those decisions aren't always represented when they are made.

Every decision affects people who deserve a seat at the table. Too often, theirs is the seat left empty.
