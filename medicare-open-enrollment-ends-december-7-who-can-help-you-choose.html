KNOW WHAT IS YOURS · UNITED STATES

Medicare's Open Enrollment Ends December 7. Who Can Help You Choose?

By Jansen / The Missing Seat · November 22, 2026

Seat Affected: People with Medicare · Seat Status: Seat At Risk

The Short Version

What happened: Medicare's yearly Open Enrollment runs October 15 to December 7, 2026, and changes start January 1. KFF, a health policy research group, counts about 4.3 million people whose plan ends on December 31.

Who it affects: People who already have Medicare and want or need to change their plan for next year. It is not the only enrollment window.

What you can do: Read your plan's 2027 notice, check your drugs, pharmacy and doctors in Medicare's Plan Finder (its online comparison tool), and talk to a free State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) counselor or 1-800-MEDICARE.

Based on: Medicare.gov and CMS enrollment rules and 2027 announcements, KFF analyses of 2027 plan endings and drug-plan premiums, the Congressional Research Service on SHIP, and a University of Michigan poll.

Full editorial

If you already have Medicare, December 7 is the last day of the yearly Open Enrollment, when you can change your coverage for 2027. It is not the deadline for everyone. People signing up for the first time, and people in some special situations, have other chances.

Four terms matter here, and they are easy to mix up. Original Medicare is Parts A and B, hospital and medical insurance. It lets you use any doctor or hospital that accepts Medicare, and it has no yearly limit on your out-of-pocket spending for covered Part A and Part B services without supplemental protection. Medigap or other coverage, such as retiree or union coverage, can help pay those costs. It does not include Part D drug coverage; you can add a stand-alone drug plan.

A Medicare Advantage plan is a Medicare-approved private plan that delivers your Part A and Part B benefits. It often adds drug coverage and extras and caps what you pay yearly for covered medical services, but it may limit you to a network and require approval for some services. Part D is the prescription-drug coverage, either a stand-alone plan added to Original Medicare or built into a Medicare Advantage plan. Medigap is a separate policy that helps pay costs Original Medicare leaves to you. It is not part of the December 7 choice, but check its rules before you leave Medicare Advantage.

During the window you can join, switch or drop a Medicare Advantage plan, move between Medicare Advantage and Original Medicare, or join, switch or drop a drug plan if you have Original Medicare. Medicare says the plan must get your request by December 7. If you are happy and your plan is continuing, Medicare says you do not have to do anything. That advice holds only if the plan's 2027 costs still suit you.

More plans are ending this year. KFF, a health policy research group, counts about 4.3 million people whose Medicare Advantage or stand-alone drug plan ends on December 31, and 1.4 million more in plans being merged into another plan from the same insurer. KFF named Humana, UnitedHealth Group, CVS Health and Aware Integrated as the parents of about half the people in ending Medicare Advantage drug plans; its count does not say why the plans are ending. People in an ending Medicare Advantage plan must choose another one or are moved automatically to Original Medicare. People in an ending drug plan need to pick a new one.

Drug plan premiums are changing too. KFF reports that no stand-alone drug plan will have a $0 premium in 2027, though about 4.1 million people without Extra Help, the federal program that helps people with limited income pay for drug coverage, paid nothing in 2026. KFF estimates that 76 percent of the 13.6 million stand-alone enrollees without Extra Help, 10.4 million people, will pay more if they keep their plan, and 2.4 million would pay more than $50 a month extra. KFF links part of this to a temporary program of extra federal payments to those plans, which it says was established in 2024 and renewed in 2025 for 2026. CMS, the federal agency that runs Medicare, announced July 28 that the program ends after 2026 because the plan companies now have enough experience under the redesigned drug benefit. KFF says the program worked as intended and, citing GAO, puts its cost at $9.8 billion over 2025 and 2026.

CMS reads the year differently. It projects average stand-alone drug premiums moving from $35.09 to $36 a month, and says about eight in ten Medicare Advantage members can remain in their current plan at the same or a lower premium. KFF's estimate, which assumes people keep their plans, is $51, and KFF expects the real figure to be lower once people switch. The $36 and $51 figures rest on different assumptions, and CMS's eight-in-ten claim covers premium only.

What happens if you miss December 7

If your plan is continuing and you do nothing, your coverage continues at that plan's 2027 prices and rules. If you are in a Medicare Advantage plan, you get one more chance: from January 1 to March 31 you can switch to another Medicare Advantage plan or go back to Original Medicare and join a drug plan. If you have Original Medicare, that window does not help you. You cannot switch to Medicare Advantage, join a drug plan or change drug plans then. After that, most people need a Special Enrollment Period, which Medicare allows for situations such as moving, losing other coverage, or getting Medicaid or Extra Help.

If your plan is ending, Medicare gives you extra time: a special period from December 8 to the last day of February. Do not count on it. If you do not join another Medicare Advantage plan before yours ends, you are enrolled in Original Medicare, which has no Part D drug coverage. Going 63 days or more without Part D or other creditable prescription-drug coverage after your Initial Enrollment Period can bring a late penalty of 1 percent of the national base drug premium Medicare sets each year, $41.33 in 2027, for each full uncovered month. It lasts as long as you have drug coverage, so a year uncovered adds about $5 a month. People who get Extra Help do not pay it.

Medigap is the item to check before you leave Medicare Advantage. Federal law gives you one six-month window to buy a Medigap policy without health questions, starting when you first have Part B at 65 or older. Outside it, insurers in most states can turn you down or charge more based on your health, and some states give more protection. If your Medicare Advantage plan leaves Medicare or stops serving your area and you return to Original Medicare, you may have a guaranteed issue right to buy specified Medigap policies. Medicare's current guidance generally gives you no more than 63 days after the Advantage coverage ends to apply. Which policies you can buy depends on the qualifying situation and your Medicare eligibility date. Keep the termination notice and ask SHIP or your state insurance department about the exact right and deadline before changing coverage. A plan merger alone should not be treated as proof that you qualify.

Who helps you choose

State Health Insurance Assistance Programs, or SHIPs, are state-based counseling programs. Their counselors give free help comparing plans, applying for Extra Help and filing appeals, and Medicare's own contact page sends people to them. A preliminary November 6 presentation from the Medicare Payment Advisory Commission (MedPAC), which advises Congress, says SHIP counselors do not receive compensation from insurers. Agents and brokers can help too. SHIP advises getting what any agent tells you in writing, and generally must agree with you on a form describing the appointment's scope at least 48 hours beforehand. Exceptions apply, so check the current rules for your situation.

In a University of Michigan poll released October 15, 60 percent of enrollees aged 65 and older said they spent less than an hour choosing coverage during last year's Open Enrollment. Among those who reviewed their options, 25 percent named Medicare's plan-finding tool or hotline as their most important source, 26 percent an insurance broker, 21 percent a plan's website and 5 percent a free local counselor like those in SHIP. The poll cannot say whether quick choices were bad choices.

Nearly half of SHIP's 10,043 staff and volunteers, according to the Congressional Research Service's count for July to December 2025, are unpaid volunteers. Federal discretionary funding was $55,242,000 in each of fiscal years 2024, 2025 and 2026. Whether that capacity meets this season's demand is not yet known.

Pull Up A Chair

Start with the paperwork already in your house. Medicare says your plan sends an Annual Notice of Change, typically by September 30, telling you how costs and benefits change for 2027. Find it, or call the plan and ask. Write down three lists: every prescription with its dose, the pharmacy you use, and the doctors and hospitals you would hate to lose.

Put them into Medicare's Plan Finder at Medicare.gov/plan-compare and look at the total estimated cost, not just the premium. A $0 Medicare Advantage plan still leaves you paying the Part B premium, the monthly charge for medical insurance, though some plans help pay part of it. For 2027, CMS has set the standard Part B premium at $209.40 per month, up $6.50 from $202.90 in 2026. The annual Part B deductible is $292, up from $283. These are Part B amounts; they are separate from Part A hospital cost sharing and Part D drug-plan costs. Higher-income beneficiaries may pay a larger Part B premium. Call your doctors' offices to confirm they take the plan. If your coverage comes through an employer or union, call your benefits administrator before you change anything.

Book free help now. Find your state's SHIP at shiphelp.org or call 1-877-839-2675. Medicare's own line, 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227, TTY 1-877-486-2048 for people with hearing loss), offers help 24 hours a day, seven days a week, except some federal holidays. If you are helping a parent, sit with them for the call. Decide by December 1 if you can, and keep the confirmation.

Seat Summary

Open Enrollment ends December 7. For people whose plan is ending or whose drug-plan premium is rising, keeping the default may cost more. Free counselors who are not paid by insurers can compare options, and nearly half of them are volunteers.

Final Thought

Set aside an hour before December 7. Bring the three lists, call a free counselor, and ask what changes for you if you do nothing.
