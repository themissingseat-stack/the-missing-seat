RACE, POWER & HISTORY • AUGUST 5, 2026

Power Is Not Proof of Superiority

“If white people are not superior, how could they oppress Black people for so long?” The question confuses the ability to dominate with evidence of greater human worth. History does not support that conclusion.

Seat Affected: The Seat of Equal Humanity
Seat Status: Denied by a hierarchy built through power
Location: Across the African diaspora and the Americas
Theme: Race, domination, historical power and human equality

The answer is straightforward:

Having power is not evidence of superiority. It is evidence that someone possessed enough military, political, legal and economic power to maintain domination.

Those are two completely different claims.

First: What Does “Superior” Mean?

Superior in what sense—morally, intellectually, physically, technologically, militarily or politically?

A group may possess more ships, guns, money, land, legal authority or political organization at a particular time. That can help explain conquest. It does not prove that every person classified within that group is innately more intelligent, creative, ethical or capable than everyone placed outside it.

Winning a war proves that one side won a war. Controlling a legislature proves control of a legislature. Writing the law proves possession of lawmaking power. None of those facts converts political victory into biological rank.

Modern biological anthropology rejects the idea that conventional racial categories divide humanity into fixed biological types arranged in a natural hierarchy. Race has real social consequences because institutions make it consequential—not because “white” and “Black” identify separate levels of humanity.

Domination Is a System, Not a Talent Test

Oppression does not place two populations into a fair contest and reveal which is “better.” It changes the conditions of the contest.

The people with power can decide who may own land, carry weapons, vote, testify, travel, learn to read, receive wages, inherit property, obtain a patent, enter a profession, sit on a jury or appeal to a court. They can criminalize resistance and use police, armies, militias, prisons and private violence to enforce those rules.

In the United States, slavery and racial hierarchy were sustained by law as well as violence. Enslaved people were treated as property. Fugitive-slave laws assisted their capture. Literacy and movement could be restricted. After emancipation, Black Codes, segregation, disfranchisement, racial terror and unequal administration of law helped preserve white control.

That is not a measurement of merit. It is an architecture of power.

History Is Full of Minority Rule

South Africa’s apartheid government maintained rule by a white minority over a Black majority. Colonial regimes often governed populations far larger than the settlers and officials controlling them. Dictators can rule millions with the assistance of a comparatively small military, party structure or security apparatus.

No serious analysis concludes that the smaller ruling group must therefore be innately superior to everyone it controls. The explanation lies in institutions, weapons, resources, alliances, coercion and the ability to divide opposition—not in a biological hierarchy.

Longevity changes nothing. An unjust system can endure because it reproduces its advantages: accumulated wealth purchases influence; political control shapes law; law protects property; education teaches the hierarchy; violence punishes resistance; and each generation inherits an unequal starting position.

Duration proves durability. It does not prove legitimacy.

The Argument Uses the Crime as Its Own Evidence

The claim contains a circular logic:

  1. One group seizes power and denies another group equal rights and resources.
  2. The resulting inequality is then presented as proof that the excluded group deserved its position.
  3. That supposed inferiority is used to justify continuing the system that produced the inequality.

The oppression creates the evidence later offered in defense of the oppression.

If a government bars a population from schools and then points to unequal literacy, the disparity does not prove incapacity. If law prevents wealth accumulation and later cites a wealth gap, the gap does not validate the law. If inventors cannot own patents and their names disappear, their absence from patent records does not prove an absence of invention.

Slavery Required Black Ability

The history of slavery contradicts the mythology used to defend it.

Enslaved Africans and their descendants were relied upon as farmers, herders, blacksmiths, carpenters, builders, sailors, cooks, artisans, healers, engineers and skilled agricultural workers. Their coerced labor powered industries in both the North and South and produced cotton, rice, indigo, tobacco and sugar for international markets.

They brought knowledge with them. Historical institutions document African expertise in cultivation and skilled trades. Enslavers extracted that knowledge while denying the people who possessed it education, ownership, compensation and public credit.

Black innovation continued under conditions designed to conceal it. Enslaved inventors could be denied patent recognition; white enslavers could claim the economic benefit; and countless improvements made in workshops, fields and homes entered use without the inventor’s name surviving in an official record.

The contradiction is obvious: a society cannot truthfully portray people as incapable while relying on their intelligence, craftsmanship, endurance, creativity and knowledge to build its wealth.

But Survival Is Not the Test of Humanity

Black people do not have to prove equality by pointing to extraordinary achievement under oppression. Human equality is not earned through inventions, wealth, military service, cultural influence or the ability to survive torture.

The achievements matter because they disprove a historical lie. They should not become a new condition placed upon human dignity.

A person who invented nothing still possessed the right not to be enslaved. A person who could not resist still possessed the right not to be beaten, raped, sold or separated from family. Human rights do not belong only to the exceptional.

What Long Domination Actually Proves

Centuries of racial oppression demonstrate that:

  • violence can be organized and legalized;
  • wealth extracted from one population can strengthen the power used against it;
  • institutions can preserve advantages long after their original justification is discredited;
  • propaganda can convert political categories into claims of natural hierarchy;
  • ordinary people can benefit from systems they did not personally create;
  • resistance can be punished without being extinguished; and
  • an injustice can survive for generations without becoming true.

Power explains how oppression was maintained. It does not transform the oppressor into a superior human being.

The Strongest Counterargument

A critic may answer that superior weapons, navigation, military organization or state capacity are themselves forms of superiority.

They may be advantages in specific capabilities at a specific moment. Precision matters. A naval advantage is not proof of intellectual superiority in every field. Greater firepower is not moral superiority. A stronger state is not evidence that every member of its favored population is biologically superior to the people it conquers.

Technology also travels. Empires borrow knowledge, exploit internal conflicts, capture labor and resources, and build later victories upon earlier conquest. Historical advantage is cumulative; it is not a laboratory test of racial worth.

Who Is Missing?

The millions whose labor, knowledge and resistance are used as evidence of an empire’s greatness while their names are removed from the achievement.

The enslaved inventors who could not claim ownership. The skilled workers recorded only as property. The African farmers whose expertise increased plantation profits. The families who preserved knowledge that official institutions refused to recognize. The people whose exclusion was later cited as proof that they had contributed nothing.

One Action

Whenever domination is offered as proof of superiority, ask:

  1. What specific advantage made domination possible?
  2. Who controlled the law, weapons, land, money and historical record?
  3. Which rights and opportunities were denied to the dominated population?
  4. Is the speaker describing a temporary power advantage—or making an unsupported claim about innate human worth?

Do not allow power to disguise itself as proof.

Hope

Systems that once presented themselves as permanent have fallen. Slavery was abolished. Colonial empires lost formal control. Apartheid ended. Segregation laws were defeated.

Their collapse did not suddenly make the oppressed equal. It exposed the truth that had existed all along: the hierarchy depended upon enforcement.

Return to the Seat

“If white people were not superior, how could they oppress Black people for so long?”

Because oppression does not require superiority.

It requires power—and enough people, laws and institutions willing to preserve it.

Power can explain domination. It cannot prove superiority.

Seat Affected: The Seat of Equal Humanity — the right to be judged as a human being, not ranked by a hierarchy built to justify conquest, exploitation and exclusion.
