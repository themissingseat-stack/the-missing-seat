## 1. Germany's new debt-advice law (published November 6)

Article: https://themissingseat.org/germany-new-debt-advice-law-starts-november-20.html
Timing: post before November 20, when most of the law starts.
Legal facts rechecked November 15: Schuldnerberatungsdienstegesetz of October 12, 2026 (BGBl. 2026 I Nr. 293); main provisions in force November 20, 2026; the reporting duty in section 4 already in force since October 16.

### Facebook, English

Germany has a new debt-advice law, and most of it starts on November 20. For anyone holding a letter they cannot pay, the practical questions are simple. Where do I go, what will it cost, and how soon can someone help?

The law, called the Schuldnerberatungsdienstegesetz, covers access to independent debt advice. It separates that advice from certain lenders and other credit companies, because arranging how a debt gets collected is a different job from helping the person who owes it. It does not promise that every service will be free. Advice centres may charge fees, as long as the fees are not an unreasonable burden, do not serve profit-making, and are explained before you use the service.

It also does not promise a quick appointment. As one example, Verbraucherzentrale Hamburg says its usual waiting time is almost four months once your documents are complete, and it lists shorter routes for urgent problems. That is one provider, not a national figure.

What to do: contact an independent advice service now, not after the deadline on your letter. Ask about eligibility, cost, waiting time and urgent help. And do not treat an appointment request as an objection or a pause in enforcement. A court or enforcement letter has its own deadline.

Beratung durch bestimmte Kreditgeber und andere Kreditunternehmen zählt nicht dazu, denn die Einziehung einer Schuld zu regeln ist etwas anderes, als dem Menschen zu helfen, der sie schuldet. ## 2. A child can have a credit file before they have a job (published November 10)

Article: https://themissingseat.org/a-child-can-have-a-credit-file-before-they-have-a-job.html
Country: United States only.

### Facebook, English

A bill addressed to a child should make an adult stop and check. A child can end up with a credit file because of identity theft, a mistake, or a legitimate link such as being an authorized user on an adult's account. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau says children generally do not have credit reports, so a file is a reason to investigate.

Federal law gives families a free protection before a problem shows up. For a child under 16, a parent or guardian can ask for a credit freeze at no cost, even if the child has no credit history yet. Sixteen- and seventeen-year-olds can request and remove a freeze themselves.

The catch is the paperwork. You have to ask each of the three nationwide credit bureaus separately, each has its own steps, and each wants proof that you are the parent or guardian. One request does not cover all three.

Start with the Federal Trade Commission's guide to child identity theft. If an account is already fraudulent, follow its recovery steps too.

## 3. Charles Drew and the blood system (published November 11)

Article: https://themissingseat.org/charles-drew-helped-build-a-blood-system-and-challenged-its-rules.html
Country: United States only.

### Facebook, English

Charles Drew is often remembered in a sentence. The record supports something much more specific.

In 1940, Drew took charge of the Blood for Britain project in New York, which collected and processed plasma for shipment during the war. His contribution was organizational as much as scientific: putting uniform collection and processing procedures into practice across participating hospitals. The Red Cross dates the start of its National Blood Donor Service to February 4, 1941, with Drew as medical director. Calling him a blood-banking pioneer is supported. Saying he invented blood transfusion is not, because earlier researchers had developed the basic methods.

The record also shows a conflict. The National Library of Medicine describes military requirements that first excluded Black donors and later required their blood to be separated by race. Drew opposed those policies as unscientific and insulting.

One more correction is worth making. A popular story says he died because a hospital refused him a transfusion. The National Library of Medicine's biography rejects that account: he received emergency care, and his injuries were too severe. The discrimination he faced is documented well enough without it.

Teach the actual work, and check an account of his death before repeating it. Full story with sources: [link]

## 4. Alice Coachman (published November 8)

Article: https://themissingseat.org/alice-coachman-was-a-champion-before-the-olympic-medal.html
Country: United States only.

### Facebook, English

Alice Coachman did not arrive at the 1948 Olympics as a beginner who happened to make history. She arrived with a record. USA Track & Field credits her with ten consecutive national high-jump titles from 1939 through 1948.

In London she cleared 1.68 meters, an Olympic and American record. Britain's Dorothy Tyler cleared the same height, but Coachman did it on her first attempt and Tyler needed two. Coachman won the gold and became the first Black woman from any country to win an Olympic gold medal.

The famous first is true. It is also only part of what she did. Her national titles, her relay running at Tuskegee Institute and her later work in education belong in the story too.

Teach the performance next to the milestone, and ask what practical support the children near you need to take part in sport. Full story with sources: [link]

## 5. Niger and river blindness (published November 7)

Article: https://themissingseat.org/niger-stopped-river-blindness-transmission-one-programme-helped-another.html
Country: Niger. Not a US or Germany post; keep it as an English post only.

### Facebook, English

Niger was the first African country verified by the World Health Organization for eliminating transmission of river blindness. WHO announced it on January 30, 2025, and its October 2026 report still lists the result.

River blindness is caused by a parasite spread by blackfly bites. It can damage skin and eyesight, so stopping it matters to people who live and work near the affected rivers.

WHO describes how it happened in stages. Niger treated blackfly breeding sites with insecticide from 1976 to 1989. Later, a programme aimed at a different disease, lymphatic filariasis, delivered ivermectin and albendazole from 2008 to 2019. Because the treatment areas overlapped, ivermectin helped interrupt the remaining river blindness transmission too. Surveys then tested blackflies and children, and tests of 16,406 children aged five to nine in 2018 and 2019 were negative.

The result depended on the health ministry, thousands of community volunteers, researchers and lab teams, and international partners.

When you hear about a health programme, ask what it delivered, how the result was checked and what happens afterward. Full story with sources: [link]

## 6. Raye Montague (published November 5)

Article: https://themissingseat.org/raye-montague-helped-change-ship-design-teach-the-work.html
Country: United States only.

### Facebook, English

Raye Montague started her civilian Navy career as a clerk typist. She went on to help move naval ship design into computing.

The Navy's historical account credits her with a computer-generated draft for the Oliver Hazard Perry-class frigate in 1971, completed in 18 hours and 26 minutes. A draft is an early design that still has to be developed, tested and refined, so it is not a finished warship. Describing it accurately makes the achievement easier to appreciate, not smaller. The Navy's account says she learned computer operation on the job and took engineering and programming classes at night. Later recognition includes a named technical centre and Arkansas's 2025 American Innovation dollar.

Her story also asks something of employers. Someone hired for one kind of work may be capable of much more, and a paid, supported chance to show it is how that gets found out.

Teach the documented work, not only the name on the poster. Full story with sources: [link]
