All Three Can Be True

Gratitude without complacency. Growth without self-rejection. Joy without pretending life is always easy.

Deutsche Übersetzung · neu

Dankbarkeit, ohne sich mit allem abzufinden. Wachstum, ohne sich selbst abzulehnen. Freude, ohne so zu tun, als wäre das Leben immer leicht.

A thought to take with you

Being thankful for what you have doesn't mean you have to stop asking for better. Wanting to grow doesn't require treating your past self as a mistake. And joy isn't a denial of what hurts. It can be one reason you keep going while you work on what needs changing.

Today, name one thing you appreciate, one thing you'd like to change, and one thing that brings you joy. All three can be true at once.

Ein Gedanke für heute

Für das dankbar zu sein, was du hast, heißt nicht, dass du aufhören musst, dir Besseres zu wünschen. Dich weiterentwickeln zu wollen, bedeutet nicht, dein früheres Ich als Fehler zu betrachten. Und Freude leugnet nicht, was wehtut. Sie kann ein Grund sein, weiterzumachen, während du veränderst, was verändert werden muss.

Nenne heute eine Sache, für die du dankbar bist, eine, die du ändern möchtest, und eine, die dir Freude macht. Alle drei dürfen gleichzeitig wahr sein.

Oct 28The Part We SkipUseful Perspective

October 28, 2026 · Jansen Bowden's words, developed with AI assistance · Useful Perspective

People rarely act before they understand.

We ask people to care, speak up, vote, volunteer, or change their minds. But sometimes we haven't even explained what happened. Start there. Say what changed, who it affects, and what someone can actually do. Then give them room to decide.

An explanation isn't the whole job. It's the door.

Oct 22You Are Allowed to Be a BeginnerPersonal Growth

October 22, 2026 · Written for this section with AI assistance · Personal Growth

Remember when you could try something just because it looked fun?

Somewhere along the way, many of us started treating every new interest like a job interview. If we can't do it well, make money from it, or explain why it's useful, we wonder whether it's worth doing.

But you are allowed to be a terrible dancer, a slow language learner, a gardener whose first basil plant doesn't survive, or a cook who needs the recipe open every thirty seconds.

Being a beginner isn't embarrassing. It's evidence that you haven't stopped exploring.

Of course, some things require training and care. Don't experiment with somebody else's safety. But in the harmless corners of life, give yourself permission to be awkward.

This week, try one thing you have no intention of mastering. Draw badly. Sing off-key. Ask someone to show you how they do something.

You don't need to be impressive to be interested. And not everything you love has to become something you're good at.

Oct 21You Can Change Your MindPerspective

October 21, 2026 · Written for this section with AI assistance · Perspective

At some point, you believed something with your whole heart. Maybe it was a plan, a friendship, a job, or a version of yourself you worked very hard to become.

Then life happened. You learned more. You noticed something you had missed. And suddenly the choice that once made sense doesn't fit quite the same way.

That doesn't automatically make the old choice foolish. It may simply mean you know something today you didn't know then.

Of course, changing your mind isn't an excuse to walk away from promises without considering who else is affected. Be honest. Explain what changed. Own your part.

But don't stay loyal to a decision only because you've already spent years defending it.

Here's a question worth asking today:

If I were choosing now, knowing what I know, would I choose the same thing?

If the answer is no, you don't have to solve everything by tonight. You can start by admitting it to yourself.

Growth isn't always becoming more certain. Sometimes it's being willing to be wrong.

Oct 20An Apology Without a “But”Life & Relationships

October 20, 2026 · Written for this section with AI assistance · Life & Relationships

An apology can be five minutes long and still avoid the one thing that matters: taking responsibility.

“I'm sorry you felt that way” puts the problem in somebody else's feelings. “I'm sorry, but I was stressed” can turn an explanation into an excuse.

Try something simpler:

“I was wrong to speak to you that way. I understand why it hurt. Next time, I'll handle it differently.”

You can explain what was happening in your life later. First, let the other person know you heard the harm, not just the complaint.

And here's the difficult part: a sincere apology doesn't buy instant forgiveness. The other person gets to decide what they need. Trust is rebuilt by what you do afterward.

Is there someone you've been meaning to speak to? You don't need a perfect speech. Start with what you did, what you understand now, and what you'll change.

Sometimes the most powerful word in an apology is the one you leave out: but.

Oct 19Use the Good CupsLife Tip

October 19, 2026 · Written for this section with AI assistance · Life Tip

There's a cup in the cupboard you save for company. A shirt you're keeping for the right occasion. A recipe that somehow never makes it to an ordinary Wednesday.

I understand the instinct. We want good things to last. But sometimes saving something special turns into never letting ourselves enjoy it.

Use the good cup for your morning coffee. Put fresh flowers in the chipped vase. Make the meal even if the only person at the table is you. None of this requires money or a perfect day. It just requires noticing what you already have.

Of course, some things really are worth preserving. A family keepsake is different from a pair of candles gathering dust. The point isn't to waste what matters. It's to stop acting as though everyday life doesn't count.

Today, choose one small pleasure you've been postponing. Give it a place in the day you actually have.

The occasion is here. You're in it.

Oct 18One More QuestionLife Tip

October 18, 2026 · Written for this section with AI assistance · Life Tip

Sometimes we're so sure we know what someone meant that we stop listening to what they actually said.

A late reply becomes a story about indifference. A short answer becomes a story about anger. A disagreement becomes a story about disrespect.

Maybe. But maybe not.

Before you build a whole story around one moment, try asking one more question.

“Did I understand you correctly?” “Is something going on?” “Would you like me to listen, or help you solve it?”

You don't have to excuse bad behavior or ignore your own boundaries. Curiosity isn't surrender. It's simply a chance to find out whether the problem is what happened or what we assumed happened.

And if the answer really is disappointing? At least you're responding to something real, not a story you wrote alone.

Today, give one conversation the gift of a second question. You might be surprised what opens up.

Oct 17Hello, self!Reflection

October 17, 2026 · Jansen Bowden's own words · Reflection

Good morning, you wonderful, complicated, occasionally impossible human being!

How did you sleep? How are you feeling? And what would make you happy today?

Before we start worrying about everybody else, let's spend a moment appreciating us.

We've stumbled, made mistakes, loved the wrong people, trusted the wrong things, worried about tomorrow, and forgotten to enjoy today.

But we've also laughed until we cried, loved beyond reason, learned things we never expected, and survived days we thought would never end.

So, hello again, self!

I'm glad you're here. I'm glad we're still curious. And I'm especially glad we're not finished becoming who we want to be.

Now, what shall we do with this wonderful day of ours?
