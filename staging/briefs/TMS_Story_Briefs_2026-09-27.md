# The Missing Seat: Three Verified Story Briefs

## 1. They Served the United States. The Nearest VA Care May Be an Ocean Away.

**Seat Affected:** Veterans living in the Federated States of Micronesia, the Marshall Islands and Palau

**Hook:** Citizens of these nations may enlist in the U.S. military, but veterans living there can face long-distance travel for VA healthcare.

**Story:** A GAO report released September 25, 2026 found that veterans in the Freely Associated States face serious barriers to VA care, including distance from facilities. Congress authorized expanded services in 2024. VA declined to implement them in 2025, then said in 2026 that it was pursuing partial implementation, including telehealth, medicine delivery, reimbursements and travel benefits. Those steps are plans in progress, not proof that care is now reaching veterans.

**Takeaway:** Permission to provide care is not the same thing as care delivered.

**Action:** Veterans and families in the three nations can document travel time, out-of-pocket costs, delayed treatment and denied reimbursement, then submit those receipts to VA, their national government and congressional veterans’ committees.

**Source:** [U.S. GAO, GAO-26-109152, September 25, 2026](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-109152)

## 2. Rural Maternal Health Got $54.6 Million. Now Show Arkansas the Results.

**Seat Affected:** Pregnant people, infants and rural families in Arkansas

**Hook:** A large grant announcement sounds like success. Families need to know whether it produces appointments, clinicians and safer births.

**Story:** CMS announced $54.6 million on September 24, 2026 for Arkansas rural health projects that include maternal healthcare, chronic-disease prevention and workforce development. The award is a verified funding commitment. It is not yet evidence that maternal services expanded or health outcomes improved.

**Takeaway:** Public money should be followed from the announcement to the patient.

**Action:** Arkansas residents can ask the state to publish a county-by-county plan showing where maternal services will open, when they will begin, how many patients will be served and which outcomes will be reported.

**Source:** [Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, September 24, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/trump-administration-announces-54.6-million-fight-chronic-disease-expand-maternal-healthcare)

Ally = Action. 🪑

## 3. Tribal Communities Are Solving Homelessness Without a Complete Federal Picture.

**Seat Affected:** American Indian and Alaska Native people experiencing housing instability or homelessness

**Hook:** Tribes are operating shelters and support services while the federal government is still working to establish a reliable national account of the need.

**Story:** GAO’s preliminary observations, released September 16, 2026, describe emergency shelters, transitional housing and supportive services run by tribes and tribal organizations. Because the findings are preliminary, they should not be treated as GAO’s final conclusions. The immediate reporting question is whether federal housing systems can identify unmet need and direct resources without forcing tribal communities into data systems that miss local realities.

**Takeaway:** A program cannot be accountable for closing a housing gap if the government cannot clearly measure the gap.

**Action:** Tribal members and housing organizations can review the preliminary report, preserve local wait-list and service-denial data, and submit corrections or missing evidence before GAO completes its work.

**Source:** [U.S. GAO, GAO-26-109173, September 16, 2026](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-109173)
